## What was established offline (saved arms and states)

1. **Realized tau on the ridge is a run-length artefact, not a target to predict.** Over 300 draws the
   ridge coordinates (v_Load*, K, sv, t0_Load*) report tau 100-200 in every arm because the three chains
   never traverse the ridge: each chain's mean shifts 1.5-4.5 within-window sd over 400 iterations and the
   spread between chains does not close (`drift2.R`). Predicting that number from one block is not a
   well-posed problem; ranking the ridge as slowest is, and the rest-of-iteration rho already does it
   (tau0 200-1000 before moves).
2. **Variance is not the missing piece.** The pooled variance over the stage so far is only 1.0-1.2x the
   block's on the slowest coordinates (`evalvar.R`), so a longer variance window changes nothing.
3. **Any lag-L / block-means kappa from the window is spurious on mixing coordinates.** With three chains
   the chain means differ by their sampling noise, which at lag 25 reads as tau 12-25 on coordinates whose
   lag-1 tau is 1-2 (`coords.R`; `.emc_alloc_kappa` from window lag-L jumps gave kappa 5-18 on lsd
   coordinates). The stage-Geyer kappa in the handoff's working tree does the same at 400 draws: it made
   lsd.v_LoadHigh (kappa 12) bind at tau 393 instead of the ridge (`replay_detail.R`, `stagekappa_p2`).
   Both kappa versions were dropped.
4. **The allocator's lag-1 prediction is right at lag 1.** On every group coordinate the plan's predicted
   lag-1 rho matches the window's whole-iteration lag-1 rho (`coords.R`). What fails is the AR(1)
   extrapolation on the ridge: one shear round moves 10-30% of the pooled variance, but rounds saturate
   within an iteration (net move per iteration = one round's) and only 25-40% of the iteration's jump
   persists to lag 25. More rounds cannot move the ridge faster; nothing in the move set can, in 400
   iterations.
5. **Why "point" cost 50% more than "new"**, from replays at the checkpoint and the saved block-4 states:
   - At the checkpoint the HMC options are a near-tie (none Inf, 0 = 126, 0.5 = 130, 1 = 131 per chain);
     summing over chains flips the shared choice to regime 1 at +30% cost, which is what the point arms ran
     in block 1 (40,400 transitions, 16.5 CPU-s/it).
   - Afterwards the group credit's settle step ("keep every particle step") wins against standing particle
     steps down, so q = 1 for every subject (`point_p2`: 0 of 101 stood down; with the credit off, 11; at
     the checkpoint, 50). The particle steps are 8 of 12-13 CPU-s per iteration.
   - "new" avoided both by accident: the lower bound made HMC look sufficient to reach the target.

## The candidate (working tree)

bf677e14 plus:
- `.emc_alloc_rho` point estimate (from the handoff) and the held-HMC solve fix (from the handoff).
- **No lag-1 group credit for subject moves** (`emc2.alloc_group_credit`, default off; the regimes,
  `.emc_alloc_credit`/`dev_ratio`/`settle` only run with it on). The attribution showed subject moves
  carry about nothing of the persistent transport on the slow group directions, and a lag-1 credit
  rewards bounce.
- **Deviation rows** (`.emc_alloc_dev_rows`): each subject's deviations from the group mean join its unit
  as coordinates with their own measured particle and HMC factors (p_dev, h_dev). The group mixes on them,
  so a plan may stand a particle step down for HMC only where HMC refreshes the deviations at least as
  well. This replaces the credit as the group's protection (the gradedR failure mode).
- **Margin rule for the HMC choice** (`.emc_alloc_choose`): among options whose objective is within
  `.EMC_ALLOC_MIN_GAIN` of the best, the cheapest runs; ties by objective. Applied per chain and to the
  shared choice (`option_cost` recorded).
- Record: `pred_subject`/`pred0_subject` keep the subjects' own coordinates (p columns).

Replays (`replay_detail.R`): at the checkpoint the candidate plans like new (cost 9.8/it, 48 particle
steps stood down, HMC 0.24); at the point block-4 state it keeps the particle steps the factors say HMC
cannot replace (cost 12.7/it).

## What remains open

The ridge itself. No allocation moves it: shear rounds saturate within an iteration at the width of the
group's conditional given the subjects' residuals, and subject refresh moves that conditional slowly.
That is a move-design question (a transport that moves each subject along its own ridge direction),
not an allocation one.

## Ridge checks (after the allocator work; scripts `reverse.R`, `marginal.R`, `profile.R` in handoff_2026-10-15b/scripts)

1. **Shear moves along the ridge are not undone.** From the instrumented PM arm's per-family paths: a shear
   displacement of the group mean along u persists 95% after one iteration and 70% after 10-25; the
   regression of the following particle/group/HMC displacements on it is zero. The ridge is slow because
   steps are small (0.09 within-sd² per iteration from shear, 0.07 from the group update), not because the
   subjects pull back. The fixed-residual hypothesis is dead.
2. **Subject marginalisation by importance sampling is infeasible**: with a Laplace-conditional mixture
   proposal in 19 dimensions the weights degenerate (ESS 1 of 100 per subject); the correlated log-ratio
   sd is 4-14 nats for steps of 0.25-1 sd.
3. **The Laplace-profile marginal along u** (each subject re-optimised in its conditional at mu + t d, V
   fixed) is a bowl about 1 sd wide around each chain's position, only 1.3-2x flatter than the shear's
   fixed-residual line (chain 1: -4.7 vs -4.9 nats at t = -1; +3.4 vs +0.3 at t = +2). In the positions
   both chains cover (35-38) chain 1's profile falls while chain 3's rises: there is no single flat valley
   of the group mean. Over the last 600 checkpoint draws every chain wanders +/-3 window-sd along u (sd
   2.94 in window units) and the joint log density swings by hundreds of nats, driven by V.
4. A joint (mu, log sd) direction profile was attempted but is dominated by the hierarchical normalising
   term without the prior on V; the ridge-to-log-sd coupling is weak (|cor| <= 0.48, slopes ~0.02), so
   it was discarded.

Verdict: no joint move along u can beat about 0.5 sd per move at useful acceptance, which the shear
already attains; a marginalised move would gain at most 2x in step. The slow variable is the whole
configuration (subjects' positions along their own weakly identified tradeoffs, with V), traversed at
tau of a few hundred iterations. That is posterior geometry, not a missing move.

5. **The group covariance is not a slow mode.** log det V mixes at tau 21-43 in the sample continuations
   (39 over the checkpoint's last 600 burn/adapt draws), the log sds at median tau 19-26 with the worst
   (lsd.v.s_low) 70-115, and the within-chain correlation of the ridge with log det V is -0.4 to 0.1.
   The hundreds-of-nats swings of the hierarchical term are fast fluctuations of a well-mixing V. The
   ridge (tau 63-259) stands alone.
