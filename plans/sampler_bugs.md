# Legacy sampler bug audit

The source references below point to `/data/work/EMC2_cens_trunc2`, used here as the legacy baseline. This covers the existing subject particle/Metropolis, blocked, factor, and SEM paths; it excludes group proposal extensions.

1. **The conditional importance update was not posterior invariant.** In `new_particle()`, a random-walk proposal was centred on the retained state, so its proposal density depended on the reference particle and systematically penalised staying put. `numbers_from_proportion()` also forced at least one particle from every mixture component, although weights assumed counts drawn in the stated mixture proportions. This could inflate between-subject variance and bias pWAIC/WAIC comparisons. The fix uses an auxiliary centre with its matching extended-target density, composes independent and random-walk conditional-importance steps, and uses an ordinary multinomial allocation. Reference: `R/sampling.R:566-681, 828-831`.

2. **Blocked updates did not consistently implement sequential Gibbs updates.** Reblocking could leave per-block tuning settings sized for the old component layout; non-contiguous `par_groups` labels were passed downstream as though they were consecutive; and later blocks could still condition on earlier coordinates from the start of the sweep. This made `n_blocks > 1` fail or target the wrong conditional. The fix remaps labels, rebuilds settings to match the active blocks, updates blocks sequentially, and carries forward each block's likelihood and selected coordinates. Reference: `R/fitting.R:220-233, 484-504, 875-883`; `R/sampling.R:252-256, 598-681`.

3. **Particle-mixture tuning continued in the sample stage.** `new_particle()` called `update_pm_settings()` after every update, and that routine could keep changing mixture weights, epsilon scales, and particle counts after warmup. Retained draws therefore came from a changing particle transition. The fix skips this tuning once `stage == "sample"`. The separate subject-covariance proposal refresh uses an expanding, doubling-window schedule in the current worktree. Reference: `R/sampling.R:681-787`.

4. **Proposal variance rescaling used a variance ratio as a standard-deviation multiplier.** `prop_var_ratio` compares empirical variances, but `epsilon` scales a Cholesky factor; multiplying epsilon by the full ratio therefore squared the intended covariance adjustment. Also, individually valid covariances could yield an unusable pooled fallback or scale estimate. The fix applies the square root of a finite positive variance ratio, bounds the adjustment, checks the pooled covariance, and resets tuning when it falls back. Reference: `R/fitting.R:529-570, 597-605`.

5. **Factor conditional proposal covariances could lose dimensions, and singular estimates got random jitter.** With a one-parameter selection, factor and infinite-factor conditional inputs could drop matrix dimensions before `cov()`/`condMVN()`. When the estimate was near-singular, `condMVN()` added random diagonal noise based on raw parameter units, making proposal covariance scale-dependent and consuming sampler RNG. The fix reconstructs matrices explicitly and regularizes in standardized coordinates with a deterministic eigenvalue floor, then symmetrizes the conditional covariance. Reference: `R/variant_factor.R:295-313`; `R/variant_infnt_factor.R:280-296`; `R/statistics.R:513-539`.

6. **SEM used population moments as each subject's alpha prior.** `gibbs_step_SEM()` returned a population mean and variance that included covariate-distribution spread; `get_group_level_SEM()` then reused them for every subject, and the nuisance merge also passed `tvar` to the particle step. This mismatched the subject's conditional prior when covariates varied. The fix keeps population summaries for reporting and returns subject-specific means and covariate-conditional variances for sampling. Reference: `R/variant_SEM.R:545-588`; `R/sampling.R:326-330`.

7. **Factor and SEM Gibbs updates used stale latent scores after the particle draw.** The particle step targets alpha with eta integrated out, but the next Gibbs step updated mu using eta left over from the previous alpha, only drawing eta afterward. That ordering is inconsistent with the partially collapsed sampler. The fix redraws eta given the new alpha before updating mu, then redraws it again after mu changes. Reference: `R/variant_factor.R:210-229`; `R/variant_infnt_factor.R:191-219`; `R/variant_SEM.R:399-429`.

8. **Checkpoint compaction treated all non-sample state as shared.** `strip_duplicates()` reduced later chains to samples and restored their other fields from chain 1; proposal caches were also stripped. A checkpoint could therefore lose chain-local adaptive state and resume with reused state from another chain. The fix shares only explicit immutable fields and preserves evolving state per chain; restoration of old checkpoints warns when their lost state cannot be recovered. Reference: `R/fitting.R:1032-1053, 1098-1109`.

9. **The random-walk step size was mis-targeted, and the controller could not reach even a correct target.** `update_pm_settings()` steered each state-centred component's step `epsilon` so that the share of its particles whose importance weight exceeds the reference particle's hit `tune$p_accept` (0.2 in adapt). With the auxiliary-centre update from item 1, for a Gaussian conditional with a matched covariance, that share is about Phi(-epsilon * sqrt(d / 2)). A target of 0.2 settles at about 1.19 / sqrt(d), half the random-walk scale 2.38 / sqrt(d), whose share is Phi(-2.38 / sqrt(2)) = 0.046. The stage floors (0.6 with four components) hid this at d <= 15 and froze every chain at d ~ 70. The 0.046 target is right for the N-particle kernel: on real subject posteriors, efficiency is flat for epsilon * sqrt(d) from about 1.2 to 3.0 and the target lands inside that range. But the correct target alone mixed worse than a fixed step at 2.38 / sqrt(d), for four reasons in the controller:
   - In burn before the efficient proposals, two state-centred components (group-covariance and chains-covariance random walks) shared one conditional-IS step, so each particle's weight carried the product of both auxiliary densities. The tighter one pinned every particle to the current state. The group-covariance walk almost never wins, so its step fell to the floor, which cut the chains walk's share and drove its step to the floor too (0.49 / sqrt(d) at d = 24, against 1.96 / sqrt(d) fixed). No step can hit the target there.
   - `epsilon[1]` changes component when the efficient proposals switch on (group walk to chains walk) and inherited the floored value.
   - The update used the running share since the block started, and a gain computed for a 0.3% target (`alphaStar = 3`, about 8x too large for 0.046). The step overshot to 4-8 / sqrt(d) and rang for ~150 iterations.
   - The mixture-weight update rewards a high share, so a too-small step also inflated the random walk's mixture weight.

   The fix: each state-centred component gets its own conditional-IS step; the controller (`update_local_steps()`) uses this iteration's share, a gain matched to the target (`alphaStar = -qnorm(target / 2)`), the target Phi(-2.38 / sqrt(2)) / search_width in burn and adapt (preburn keeps its search target), and a floor of a quarter of the random-walk scale. A component restarts at 2.38 / sqrt(d) when it starts stepping on a different covariance (its layout tag changes, e.g. group to chains walk). When a covariance it already steps on is rebuilt between blocks, `update_epsilon_scale()` instead multiplies the step by sqrt(old / new mean variance), which keeps the absolute step size. Mixture weights no longer use `tune$p_accept` (item 10). Against the fixed burn step (500 burn iterations, pairs R-hat > 1.1, three seeds): lba71sim 378-392 vs 483-608, jointsim 177-219 vs 249-314, wide15 0-4 vs 1-5, freeA15 38-57 vs 13-62 (group level unconverged in both). The adapted step carries into sample. Reference: `R/sampling.R` `new_particle()`, `update_pm_settings()`, `update_local_steps()`.

10. **Mixture weights and particle counts were adapted from statistics that do not compare across components.** The mixture update divided each component's per-particle win rate by its mixture weight a second time, so its fixed point was mix proportional to sqrt(win rate / target). For the random walks the win rate is pinned at the step target by `update_local_steps()`, so their weight was set by the ratio of two constants. The win rates of separate conditional-IS steps (independence step, each local step) are also not the same quantity. The weights now move toward each component's expected squared jump per particle, in the chains metric (one likelihood evaluation per particle, so this is jump per unit cost), with no targets. The particle count follows the weight ESS of the independence step only; it used to be summed over the steps. Reference: `R/sampling.R` `update_pm_settings()`, `new_particle()`.

11. **Proposal inputs.** `chains_mu` was the mean of the window draws weighted by exp(LL). The draws are already posterior draws, so this targets prior x L^2, and its weights degenerate (effective size about (3/4)^(d/2) of the window under a Gaussian approximation; one draw by d ~ 70). It is now the plain mean. Window covariance correlations are no longer shrunk toward zero. The efficient proposal's mean follows the current group mean through the fitted regression, instead of staying at one stale conditioning draw. The Laplace proposal uses the 4x-widened prior at the burn mode start as at every refresh; its mean is that density's mode (one Newton step from the conditional mode) rather than the conditional mode, so mean and covariance describe one density; chain starts and diagnostics keep the conditional covariance. Pareto k is recomputed at every refresh. The ensemble pool's defensive t scale is 1 + 3 / sqrt(p) instead of 4. Reference: `R/fitting.R` `create_chain_proposals()`, `create_eff_proposals()`; `R/mode_start.R`; `R/ensemble_move.R`.

12. **Smaller items.** A batch likelihood failure in the particle step retries row by row and gives failing candidates zero density, instead of keeping the state with a state-dependent probability. The marginalisation warm start is frozen in the sample stage. The diagonal-gamma mu update draws jointly when the prior on mu is correlated.

13. **The stop rule's R-hat range-transformed unbounded parameters.** The stop rule's `gd_summary()` reaches `coda::gelman.diag()` through `get_summary_stat()` and `gelman_diag_robust()`, with `transform = TRUE`. That log-transforms every column whose draws are all positive, and logit-transforms every column whose draws all lie in (0, 1). Unmapped `mu` and `alpha` are on the unbounded sampling scale, so an all-positive column is a coincidence of the draws. A chain whose draws approach zero then turns into a spurious blow-up of the between-chain variance, which can hold the stop rule open long after the chains agree. On one LBA fit, sampling ran about 1,400 iterations instead of about 1,000. The fix has `get_summary_stat()` default `transform = FALSE` for unmapped `mu` and `alpha`, which `r_hat()` (in the current worktree) passes through to `gelman_diag_robust()`. `sigma2`, correlations and mapped output keep the transform, and `version = "old"` remains the default. Reference: `R/statistics.R:257` `gelman_diag_robust()` and `R/statistics.R:574` `get_summary_stat()`; `R/s3_funcs.R:1009` `gd_summary()`.
