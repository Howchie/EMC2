\begin{abstract}
This note is the mathematical specification of the Finite Reservoir Quorum race model (\textsc{FRQ}) as implemented in EMC2. In \textsc{FRQ}, evidence is a set of discrete registration events, and an accumulator responds once a quorum of them has arrived. Its first-passage law is a regularized incomplete beta function, so the likelihood is closed form. The note's central result is an exact counter reading of that law. Every \textsc{FRQ} accumulator is a Poisson counter that runs on an operational clock, has a Gamma-distributed trial intensity, and has a threshold of $K$ events. This reading gives the fitted coordinates $(K, v, sv, h)$: event threshold, mean initial input rate, between-trial SD of that rate, and eventual completion probability. A shape parameter $\cvu$ (\texttt{clock}) fixes the clock. At $sv=0$ the model reduces to an exact Poisson counter with no rate variability. The classic Poisson counter race, its gamma-mixed-rate extension and its linear self-exciting extension are all exact submodels with real-valued $K$. These were the independent-counter submodels of the former \textsc{PCOUNTER} model, which was removed; Section~\ref{sec:notcovered} lists the parts of it that \textsc{FRQ} does not reproduce. We also give the low-threshold state $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ (\texttt{plo}), a per-accumulator mixture over two thresholds that keeps the likelihood closed form and can produce fast errors, the error-RT signature of each mechanism, measured identification results, and what the kernel does not cover.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and Notation}
%======================================================================

Counting models represent decision evidence as discrete, all-or-none events in continuous time. The classic counter models come from Pike \cite{Pike1966}, LaBerge \cite{LaBerge1962} and Townsend and Ashby \cite{TownsendAshby1983}. This note treats one kernel, \textsc{FRQ}, and shows that the counter models used in the response-time literature are special cases of it.

EAMs are applied to separate latent mechanisms that can produce the same mean response time while shaping the RT distribution differently. The fitted coordinates are therefore chosen so that each one is a mechanism: threshold, rate, rate variability, evidence ceiling and threshold variability. None of them is a response-time summary.

\subsection{Response Time Decomposition}
Let $T$ be the observed response time and $t_0 \ge 0$ the non-decision time. The decision time is $u = T - t_0$. Densities $f(u)$, distribution functions $F(u)$ and survivors $S(u)=1-F(u)$ are written in decision time, and $f_T(t) = f(t - t_0) \mathbf{1}_{\{t > t_0\}}$.

\subsection{Race Architecture and Defective Distributions}
Each response alternative $i \in \{1, \dots, R\}$ has an independent accumulator with decision latency $U_i \in (0, \infty]$. The first accumulator to finish determines the response:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Response} = \arg\min_{i} (t_{0i} + U_i), \qquad T = \min_{i} (t_{0i} + U_i).
\end{equation}
The density for alternative $i$ winning at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  g_i(t) = f_i(t - t_{0i}) \prod_{j \neq i} S_j(t - t_{0j}), \label{eq:race-likelihood}
\end{equation}
with $S_j(u)=1$ for $u\le 0$. An accumulator may be defective, $F_i(\infty) < 1$, in which case it never finishes with probability $1 - F_i(\infty)$. An omission (no response at all) has probability
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{Omission}) = \prod_{j=1}^R \big(1 - F_j(\infty)\big).
\end{equation}

%======================================================================
\section{The Finite Reservoir Quorum Process}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Generative Mechanism}
An accumulator has a reservoir of $N$ potential evidence units. On each trial:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item each unit is independently \textbf{available} with probability $p \in (0, 1]$;
  \item an available unit registers after an independent delay with CDF $G(u)$, and an unavailable unit never registers;
  \item the accumulator responds once a \textbf{quorum} of $K$ units has registered.
\end{enumerate}
If fewer than $K$ units are available, the accumulator never responds. By time $u$, each unit has registered with probability $q(u) = pG(u)$. The registered count is therefore $\Binomial(N, q(u))$, and the binomial-tail identity gives
\begin{equation}
  F(u) = P\{\Binomial(N,q(u)) \ge K\} = I_{q(u)}(\alpha, \beta), \qquad \alpha = K,\quad \beta = N - K + 1, \label{eq:frq-cdf}
\end{equation}
where $I_q(a,b)$ is the regularized incomplete beta function. The density and survivor are
\begin{equation}
  f(u) = p\, g(u)\, \frac{q(u)^{\alpha - 1} (1 - q(u))^{\beta - 1}}{B(\alpha, \beta)}, \qquad
  S(u) = I_{1 - q(u)}(\beta, \alpha), \label{eq:frq-pdf}
\end{equation}
with $g = G'$. The kernel evaluates $1 - q(u) = (1-p) + p\,(1 - G(u))$ directly, which avoids cancellation when $q(u)\approx 1$.

\subsection{Registration Law}
The registration law has rate $\lambda$ and a shape $\cvu \ge 0$, the parameter \texttt{clock}. At $\cvu = 0$ it is exponential, $G(u) = 1 - \e^{-\lambda u}$. At $\cvu > 0$ each unit's rate is Gamma distributed with mean $\lambda$ and CV $\cvu$, which gives the Lomax law
\begin{equation}
  G_{\cvu}(u) = 1 - \left(1 + \cvu^2 \lambda u\right)^{-1/\cvu^2}, \qquad
  g_{\cvu}(u) = \lambda \left(1 + \cvu^2 \lambda u\right)^{-1/\cvu^2 - 1}.
\end{equation}
In every case $g(0) = \lambda$. Section~\ref{sec:counter} shows that $\cvu$ is best read as the shape of the counter's clock.

\subsection{Continuous Shapes and the Intrinsic Defect}
Equations~\eqref{eq:frq-cdf}--\eqref{eq:frq-pdf} never use integrality, so they are valid for all real $\alpha, \beta > 0$. As $u \to \infty$, $q \to p$ and
\begin{equation}
  h = F(\infty) = I_p(\alpha, \beta). \label{eq:frq-h}
\end{equation}
When $p < 1$ the accumulator is defective, with a genuine point mass $1-h$ at $+\infty$. No separate omission mixture is needed. This defect is not an additive contaminant, because $p$ also reshapes the finite-RT distribution.

%======================================================================
\section{The Counter Reading}\label{sec:counter}
%======================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Gamma-mixed Poisson counter]\label{prop:counter}
Let $G_\alpha \sim \GammaDist(\alpha, 1)$ and $W \sim \GammaDist(\beta, 1)$ be independent, and define the operational clock
\begin{equation}
  \phi(u) = \frac{q(u)}{1 - q(u)}, \qquad \phi(0) = 0,\quad \phi'(0) = p\lambda.
\end{equation}
Then $F(u) = P\{G_\alpha \le W \phi(u)\}$. For integer $\alpha = K$, this is the time of the $K$th event of a Poisson process whose cumulative intensity on the trial is $W\phi(u)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$I_q(\alpha,\beta) = P\{B \le q\}$ for $B \sim \BetaDist(\alpha,\beta)$, and $B = G_\alpha/(G_\alpha + W)$. Hence $B \le q \iff G_\alpha \le W q/(1-q)$. For integer $K$, $P\{G_K \le m\} = P\{\Poisson(m) \ge K\}$, so given $W$ the count by time $u$ is Poisson with mean $W\phi(u)$.
\end{proof}

The three FRQ ingredients then read as counter mechanisms.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Threshold.} $\alpha = K$ is the number of events required.
  \item \textbf{Between-trial rate variability.} The trial intensity $W$ has mean $\beta$ and CV $1/\sqrt{\beta}$. The mean initial input rate is $E[W]\,\phi'(0) = \beta p \lambda$.
  \item \textbf{Evidence ceiling.} For $p<1$ the clock saturates at $\phi(\infty) = p/(1-p)$. The trial then has a finite expected total of evidence, $\beta p/(1-p)$ events, and the defect $1-h$ is the probability that this total falls short of $K$.
\end{itemize}
Proposition~\ref{prop:counter} is an equality of first-passage laws, not of counting processes. At $p = 1$, $\cvu = 0$ and $N = K = 3$ ($\beta = 1$), the reservoir registers its three units at total rates $3\lambda, 2\lambda, \lambda$ (depletion), while the counter's successive event rates are $\lambda, 2\lambda, 3\lambda$ (reinforcement, Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity}). Both give $F(u) = (1 - \e^{-\lambda u})^3$. The two mechanisms are observationally equivalent when only completion times are observed.

For the same reason, $\beta p\lambda = (N - K + 1)p\lambda$ is the counter-equivalent initial rate. The reservoir's own initial aggregate registration rate is $Np\lambda$, which is larger whenever $K > 1$ (in the example above, $3\lambda$ against $\lambda$).

\subsection{Clock Shape and Contagion}
At $p = 1$, the clock is $\phi(u) = (1 + \cvu^2\lambda u)^{1/\cvu^2} - 1$. It is accelerating for $\cvu < 1$ (exponential, $\e^{\lambda u} - 1$, at $\cvu = 0$), exactly linear ($\lambda u$) at $\cvu = 1$, and decelerating for $\cvu > 1$. For $p<1$ every clock saturates at $p/(1-p)$, so "constant rate" is exact only at $h = 1$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional intensity]\label{prop:intensity}
Given that $n$ events have occurred by time $u$, the counter's intensity is
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_n(u) = (\beta + n)\, \frac{\phi'(u)}{1 + \phi(u)} = (\beta + n)\,\frac{q'(u)}{1 - q(u)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Gamma--Poisson conjugacy: $W \mid \{n \text{ events by } u\} \sim \GammaDist(\beta + n,\, 1 + \phi(u))$, and the intensity is $W\phi'(u)$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity} shows that between-trial rate variability and self-excitation are one mechanism, contagion, seen on different clocks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item at $\cvu = 0$, $p = 1$: $\lambda_n = \beta\lambda + \lambda n$, a linear pure-birth process with base rate $\nu = \beta\lambda$ and self-excitation $\gamma = \lambda$, with no time dependence;
  \item at $\cvu = 1$, $p = 1$: $\lambda_n(u) = (\nu + \lambda n)/(1 + \lambda u)$, the Pólya process of a gamma-mixed Poisson counter, where the contagion decays in time.
\end{itemize}
At $p = 1$ and any $\cvu$, the intensity is $\lambda_n(u) = v(1 + n/\beta)/(1 + \cvu^2 v u/\beta)$. At $K = 1$ this gives $F(u) = 1 - (1 + \cvu^2 v u/\beta)^{-\beta/\cvu^2}$, which depends on $\cvu$ and $\beta$ only through $\beta/\cvu^2$, so at $K = 1$ the two are exactly confounded (and at $\cvu = 0$ the law is exponential with rate $v$, free of $\beta$). For $K > 1$ the factor $1 + n/\beta$ separates them in principle, but in practice they are severely confounded (Section~\ref{sec:ident}).

\subsection{Real-Valued Thresholds}
For real $K$, Proposition~\ref{prop:counter} still defines the model: the accumulator responds when the operational time $W\phi(u)$ passes a $\GammaDist(K,1)$ level, which has mean $K$ and variance $K$. For integer $K$ this level is exactly the $K$th arrival of a unit-rate Poisson process. For non-integer $K$ it interpolates between counts and is not the first passage of an integer-valued count process. The fitted model keeps $K \ge 1$: the density near onset is proportional to $u^{K-1}$, so for $K < 1$ it is singular at $t_0$ and the likelihood is unbounded as $t_0 \to \min(\mathrm{rt})$.

\section{Fitted Coordinates \texorpdfstring{$(K, v, sv, h)$}{(K, v, sv, h)}}
%======================================================================

\begin{definition}[Counter chart]
The fitted coordinates are
\begin{equation}
  K = \alpha, \qquad v = \beta p \lambda, \qquad sv = \frac{v}{\sqrt{\beta}}, \qquad h = F(\infty),
\end{equation}
together with $t_0$, the low-state probability $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:plo}) and the clock shape $\cvu$. The rate CV $\mathit{cv} = sv/v = 1/\sqrt{\beta}$ is derived.
\end{definition}

In words, $K$ is the event threshold, $v$ is the mean initial input rate (events per second), $sv$ is the between-trial SD of the input rate, as in the LBA, and $h$ is the probability that the accumulator ever responds. The kernel inverts the chart in closed form, apart from one beta quantile:
\begin{equation}
  \beta = (v/sv)^{2}, \qquad p = Q_{\alpha,\beta}(h), \qquad \lambda = \frac{v}{\beta p}, \label{eq:chart-inverse}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\alpha,\beta}$ is the beta quantile function; with $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} > 0$ it is replaced by the root of Eq.~\eqref{eq:plo-h}. The solution depends only on $(K, sv/v, h, \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})$, and the kernel caches it on those four arguments. A rate $v$ that varies per trial therefore costs a quantile per distinct value only when $sv > 0$ and $h < 1$: at $h = 1$ there is no quantile, and at $sv = 0$ the key does not involve $v$. At $h = 1$, $p = 1$ exactly and the accumulator is proper.

In this chart, $v$ and $h$ separate ``how fast evidence arrives'' from ``how much evidence exists'', and the onset behaviour is controlled by $v$ alone: at $K = 1$, $f(0^+) = v$ for every $sv$, $h$ and $\cvu$. With $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} > 0$ and $K > 1$, $f(0^+) = \pi_{\mathrm{lo}} v$ (Section~\ref{sec:plo}).

\subsection{The CV Chart}\label{sec:cvchart}
\texttt{FRQ(parameterization = "cv")} samples the rate CV in place of $sv$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{cv} = \frac{sv}{v} = \beta^{-1/2}, \qquad sv = \mathit{cv}\, v .
\end{equation}
The map $(v, sv) \leftrightarrow (v, \mathit{cv})$ is a bijection on $v > 0$, so the two charts are the same model and give the same likelihood at $sv = \mathit{cv}\,v$; $\mathit{cv} = 0$ is the same exact branch as $sv = 0$. What differs is what a design holds fixed when $v$ varies.

\begin{proposition}[Rate as a time scale]\label{prop:timescale}
With $(K, \mathit{cv}, h, \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}, \cvu)$ fixed, the decision time scales as $T_v \overset{d}{=} T_1/v$:
\begin{equation}
  F(u; v) = F(v u; 1).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\beta = \mathit{cv}^{-2}$ is free of $v$, and so is $p$, which depends only on $(K, \beta, h, \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})$. Then $\lambda = v/(\beta p)$ is proportional to $v$, and $G(u)$, hence $q(u) = pG(u)$ and every term of $F(u)$, depends on $u$ only through $\lambda u$. In the $\mathit{cv} = 0$ branch $E$ is free of $v$ and the argument is $vu$ (at $h = 1$) or $E\,G(u)$ with $\lambda = v/E$.
\end{proof}

In the $sv$ chart the same holds only along $sv \propto v$; at fixed $sv$, a change of $v$ changes $\beta$ and therefore the shape. In a race whose accumulators share $t_0$, a common factor on every accumulator's $v$ rescales the race's decision time and leaves the choice probabilities unchanged (with unequal $t_0$ it can change which accumulator finishes first); changes in the ratio of the rates change both. The quantile key $(K, \mathit{cv}, h, \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})$ is free of $v$, so in this chart a per-trial $v$ never costs a beta quantile.

At $h = 1$ and $\cvu = 1$, $T = Y/\Lambda$ with $Y \sim \GammaDist(K, 1)$ and $\Lambda \sim \GammaDist(\beta, \text{rate } \beta/v)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{CV}^2(T) = \Big(1 + \frac{1}{K}\Big)\frac{\beta - 1}{\beta - 2} - 1 \approx \frac{1}{K} + \mathit{cv}^2 + \frac{\mathit{cv}^2}{K} \qquad (\beta > 2),
\end{equation}
the Poisson counter's $1/K$ plus the rate variability, and $P(T > t) \sim c\,t^{-\beta}$ as $t \to \infty$. In the CV chart, a shared CV (\texttt{cv \textasciitilde{} 1}) is the equal-CV model of Table~\ref{tab:share}.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$sv = 0$}{sv = 0} Limit}
\begin{proposition}[Exact Poisson counter]\label{prop:poisson}
Let $P_K(m) = P\{\GammaDist(K,1) \le m\}$ be the regularized lower incomplete gamma function. As $sv \to 0$ (so $\mathit{cv} \to 0$) with $(K, v, h)$ fixed:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if $h = 1$, then $F(u) \to P_K(v u)$, the homogeneous Poisson (Erlang for integer $K$) counter, whatever the clock shape;
  \item if $h < 1$, then $F(u) \to P_K\big(E\, G(u)\big)$ with $E = P_K^{-1}(h)$ and registration rate $\lambda = v/E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$W/\beta \to 1$ in probability as $\beta \to \infty$. When $h = 1$, $\lambda = v/\beta \to 0$ and $\beta\phi(u) \to v u$. When $h < 1$, $p \to 0$ with $\beta p \to E$, so $\beta \phi(u) = \beta p G/(1 - pG) \to E\,G(u)$, and $h = P_K(E)$ fixes $E$.
\end{proof}

Case 2 is the Poisson limit of the reservoir ($N \to \infty$, $p \to 0$, $Np \to E$): the trial holds a $\Poisson(E)$ number of available units, each registering with law $G$. Because $sv = 0$ is reachable, the kernel implements it as an exact branch rather than a limit. The constructor's default is $sv = 0$, the classic counter with no between-trial rate variability. The kernel also takes this branch whenever $\mathit{cv} < 10^{-4}$ ($\beta > 10^8$), where the general branch differs from the limit by about $5\times10^{-9}$ in the CDF and the beta functions start losing digits to the huge shape.

\subsection{Choice of Clock}
$\cvu$ is a model-variant choice, not a free parameter to estimate alongside $sv$. The default is $\cvu = 1$, the constant-rate counter, in which $\mathit{cv}$ is exactly between-trial rate variability (Section~\ref{sec:submodels}). $\cvu = 0$ is the accelerating, self-exciting variant.

%======================================================================
\section{The Low-Threshold State \texorpdfstring{$\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$}{plo}}\label{sec:plo}
%======================================================================
On each trial, each accumulator independently commits at a low threshold $K_{\mathrm{lo}}$ with probability $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ (parameter \texttt{plo}), and at $K$ otherwise. Both thresholds run on the same counter: the same trial intensity $W$, clock and availability $p$. Write $F_k$, $f_k$ and $S_k$ for the laws of Section~\ref{sec:counter} at threshold $k$ and the shared $(\beta, p, \lambda, \cvu)$. Then
\begin{equation}
  F(u) = (1 - \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})\,F_K(u) + \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}\,F_{K_{\mathrm{lo}}}(u), \label{eq:plo-mix}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $f$ and $S$, and for the $sv = 0$ branch with $P_k$ in place of $I_q(k, \beta)$. The kernel fixes $K_{\mathrm{lo}} = 1$, so the low component is elementary:
\begin{equation}
  F_1(u) = 1 - (1 - q(u))^\beta, \qquad f_1(u) = \beta\, p\, g(u)\,(1 - q(u))^{\beta - 1}, \qquad P_1(X) = 1 - \e^{-X}.
\end{equation}
$K_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is a kernel constant, not a fitted column. Every evaluator takes it from one place, so freeing it later changes only where the value comes from. At $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0$ the kernel takes the base code path.

\paragraph{Generative reading.} In the counter of Proposition~\ref{prop:counter}, write the $\GammaDist(K, 1)$ level as $E_1 + E_r$ with $E_1 \sim \GammaDist(1, 1)$ and $E_r \sim \GammaDist(K - 1, 1)$ independent. The accumulator normally finishes when $W\phi(u)$ passes $E_1 + E_r$. With probability $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ it finishes when $W\phi(u)$ passes $E_1$, that is, at its first event. For integer $K$ this is a decision on the first Poisson arrival instead of the $K$th. The draw is per accumulator, so the race likelihood still factors over accumulators (Eq.~\eqref{eq:race-likelihood}).

\paragraph{Completion probability.} Both components share $p$, so
\begin{equation}
  h = (1 - \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})\, I_p(K, \beta) + \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}\, I_p(K_{\mathrm{lo}}, \beta). \label{eq:plo-h}
\end{equation}
At $h = 1$, $p = 1$ exactly and nothing is solved. For $h < 1$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} > 0$, $p$ is the root of Eq.~\eqref{eq:plo-h}. The right side increases in $p$ and lies between the two pure completion probabilities, so the root is bracketed by the pure quantiles $Q_{K,\beta}(h)$ and $Q_{K_{\mathrm{lo}},\beta}(h) = 1 - (1-h)^{1/\beta}$. The kernel solves it in $t = \logit p$, holding $(\log p, \log(1-p))$, by Newton steps that fall back to bisection when they leave the bracket. The residual is $\log m - \log h$ for $h \le 1/2$ and $\log(1-h) - \log(1 - m)$ above, so a defect within rounding of zero keeps its digits. At $sv = 0$ the same solve gives $E$ from $h = (1 - \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})P_K(E) + \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}P_{K_{\mathrm{lo}}}(E)$, in $\log E$.

\paragraph{Onset.} For $K > 1$ only the low component has mass at onset, and $f(0^+) = \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}\, v$ for every $sv$, $h$ and $\cvu$. The leading edge becomes first order in time, $F(u) \approx \pi_{\mathrm{lo}} v u$, whatever $K$ is. The density therefore jumps to $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} v$ at $t_0$, so the likelihood is not regular in $t_0$, as it already is at $K = 1$.

\paragraph{Error signature.} The low component makes the hazard elasticity of Section~\ref{sec:ident} rise early, which is the condition for fast errors under a rate difference alone. Near onset, the error and correct densities conditional on the response tend to the ratio $(v_e/v_c)(P_c/P_e)$, so very early responses are over-represented among errors whenever $v_e/v_c > P_e/P_c$. In two-accumulator races at 80\% accuracy with $K = 6$, $K_{\mathrm{lo}} = 1$ and only $v$ differing, errors are $15\%$ and $60\%$ faster than correct responses at the median for $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0.1$ and $0.3$ ($sv = 0$), and $5\%$ and $53\%$ faster at $\mathit{cv} = 0.3$. The fixed-threshold race gives $12\%$ slower errors.

\paragraph{Identification.} At $K = K_{\mathrm{lo}}$ the two components coincide and $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ drops out of the law; just above it, $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is weakly identified.

\paragraph{Cost.} Each evaluation now computes both components. The low one is elementary, so the main incomplete beta remains the dominant term. On one 810-trial two-accumulator subject, a likelihood evaluation costs $1.29$ times the $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0$ cost at $h = 1$, and $1.19$ times with $h$ free per accumulator. The root solve adds about $0.6$--$1\,\mu$s per fresh availability key, which is under 1\% of a particle there.

\section{Classic Poisson Counters as Exact Submodels}\label{sec:submodels}
%======================================================================

Write $\nu$ for a counter's base input rate, $sv$ for the between-trial SD of that rate, and $\gamma$ for linear self-excitation, so the counter's transition rate is $\nu + \gamma n$. Every combination except $sv$ and $\gamma$ together is an exact \textsc{FRQ} submodel at $h = 1$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0$, and in each one $v = \nu$. With $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} > 0$ each CDF in Table~\ref{tab:submodels} becomes its mixture with the $K = 1$ member, Eq.~\eqref{eq:plo-mix}.

\paragraph{Gamma-mixed rate.} Given $\Lambda$, the count by time $u$ is $\Poisson(\Lambda u)$. Mixing over $\Lambda \sim \GammaDist(\beta, \beta/\nu)$ gives a negative binomial count with $P\{N(u) \ge K\} = I_{\nu u/(\beta + \nu u)}(K,\beta)$. At $p = 1$, $\cvu = 1$ and $\lambda = \nu/\beta$, \textsc{FRQ} has $q(u) = \lambda u/(1 + \lambda u) = \nu u/(\beta + \nu u)$, which is the same expression.

\paragraph{Linear pure birth.} Solving the forward equations of the birth process with rates $\nu + \gamma n$ gives a negative binomial state law with stopping parameter $\nu/\gamma$ and success probability $\e^{-\gamma u}$. The flux out of state $K-1$, under the substitution $z = 1 - \e^{-\gamma u}$, is a $\BetaDist(K, \nu/\gamma)$ density in $z$, which gives the CDF in Table~\ref{tab:submodels}. This is also Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity} at $\cvu = 0$ and $p = 1$.

\paragraph{Two-alternative Erlang race.} For integer thresholds and $sv = 0$, $h = 1$, the choice probability has the familiar negative binomial form \cite{RatcliffSmith2004}:
\begin{equation}
  P_1 = \sum_{n=0}^{K_2 - 1} \binom{K_1 + n - 1}{n} \left(\frac{\nu_1}{\nu_1 + \nu_2}\right)^{K_1} \left(\frac{\nu_2}{\nu_1 + \nu_2}\right)^n.
\end{equation}

\section{Error Signatures and Identification}\label{sec:ident}
%======================================================================

The results below were measured in two-accumulator races calibrated to 80\% accuracy. The error signature is the median error RT minus the median correct RT. Identification is reported as the per-trial posterior SD from the Fisher information; divide by $\sqrt{n}$ for $n$ trials. Unless stated otherwise, $K = 3$, $h = 1$, the clock is constant-rate ($\cvu = 1$), and the rate CV $\mathit{cv}$ is shared across accumulators (Section~\ref{sec:share} frees the SD per accumulator). Other values of $K$, $h < 1$ and the accelerating clock were not tested.

\subsection{Error Signatures by Mechanism}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rate difference with no rate variability.} On the accelerating clock with $K = 1$, the race is a race of exponentials, and correct and error RTs are identically distributed. At $K = 3$ the race alone makes errors 11--15\% slower, and on the accelerating clock $\mathit{cv}$ barely changes this, because the acceleration cancels its slow-error effect.
  \item \textbf{Between-trial rate variability, CV shared} ($\cvu = 1$): slow errors. Errors are 13\%, 16\%, 46\% and 139\% slower at $\mathit{cv} \approx 0.03$, $0.18$, $0.58$ and $1$.
  \item \textbf{Evidence-ceiling difference} (unequal $h$ at a common $v$): fast errors, 25--52\% faster than correct responses, with omission rates around $5\times10^{-4}$. The weaker accumulator runs out of evidence, so it can only win early.
  \item \textbf{Threshold below one} ($K<1$): strong fast errors, but the likelihood is unbounded at $t_0 \to \min(\mathrm{rt})$, so $K \ge 1$ is enforced.
  \item \textbf{Low-threshold state $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$:} fast errors under a rate difference alone (Section~\ref{sec:plo}).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{When errors can be fast.} In an independent race with any number of accumulators, the error density from accumulator $j$ divided by the correct density is proportional to the hazard ratio $\eta_j(u)/\eta_c(u)$, because the shared survivors cancel. Errors are slower than correct responses at every quantile exactly when that ratio rises in $u$. When accumulators differ only in $v$, every law is a time rescaling of one base law with hazard $\eta$, and
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd \log(\eta_j/\eta_c)}{\dd \log u} = \epsilon(v_j u) - \epsilon(v_c u), \qquad \epsilon(x) = \frac{\dd \log \eta(x)}{\dd \log x}.
\end{equation}
With $v_j < v_c$, errors are slow wherever $\epsilon$ falls and fast wherever it rises. On a grid of 144 \textsc{FRQ} members at $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0$ ($K \in [1, 8]$, $\mathit{cv} \in [0, 2]$, $h \in [0.5, 1]$, all three clocks), $\epsilon$ never rises. A rate difference alone therefore gives slow errors at every quantile. Fast errors need either a shape that differs between accumulators (unequal $h$, $\mathit{cv}$ or $K$), or a base law whose log-hazard is convex in log time somewhere, such as an early, near-exponential component (low $K$) mixed with a later one.

The kernel therefore separates two mechanisms cleanly: a rate difference with a shared rate CV gives slow errors, with size set by $\mathit{cv}$, and an evidence-ceiling difference gives fast errors. With a rate SD per accumulator, the sign depends on their ratio (Section~\ref{sec:share}).

Each mechanism was fitted alone, never together, and each model was fitted to the other's exact likelihood to measure distinguishability.

\subsection{Clock Shape Versus Rate CV}
With the clock fixed at $\cvu = 1$, the full race identifies the rate variability: the per-trial SD of $\log\beta$ is 4.2, about 0.19 at 500 trials. With $\cvu$ and the rate variability both free, their posterior correlation is 1.000 and $\operatorname{SD}(\log\beta)$ is 263. Both produce the same contagion signature (Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity}). At $K = 1$ they are exactly confounded, since only $\beta/\cvu^2$ enters the law. At $K = 3$ they are distinct in principle, but this design cannot separate them in practice. The constructor fixes $\cvu = 1$.

\section{What the Kernel Does Not Cover}\label{sec:notcovered}
%======================================================================
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rate variability and self-excitation together.} For integer $K$ the law is a finite sum, which \textsc{PCOUNTER} evaluated by a Stirling recurrence. That sum does not extend to real $K$, and no closed form for real $K$ is known. Intermediate clocks ($0 < \cvu < 1$) come within $10^{-3}$ nats per trial of it, but their parameters do not split into separate $sv$ and $\gamma$. Both are contagion, so the joint model would be weakly identified in any case.
  \item \textbf{Geometric threshold excess} $\omega$. It was omitted on the evidence of Table~\ref{tab:cv-omega}.
  \item \textbf{Rates correlated across accumulators.} The accumulators race independently. The removed \textsc{PCOUNTERcorr} model used a shared-component gamma construction, and its derivation is in \texttt{Math/PCOUNTER\_correlated\_rates.md}. An \textsc{FRQ} version would need quadrature over the shared factor. The old implementation can be recovered from git at commit \texttt{75582521}.
  \item \textbf{Log-odds threshold frailty $\delta$.} An earlier extension multiplied each accumulator's completion odds by $\e^{\varepsilon}$, $\varepsilon \sim \operatorname{Uniform}(-\delta, \delta)$, giving the closed form $F_\delta = H_\delta(F)$ with $H_\delta(z) = [\log(1 + \e^{x+\delta}) - \log(1 + \e^{x-\delta})]/(2\delta)$, $x = \logit z$. It was replaced by $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$. $\delta$ scales both tails by $\sinh(\delta)/\delta$ without changing the onset order or the tail exponent, spreads the bulk only at second order, and is confounded with $K$ rather than rate CV: the best $(K, v, \mathit{cv})$ mimic of a $\delta$ truth at $K = 3$ sets $\mathit{cv} = 0$ and lowers $K$ to $2.77$, $2.28$ and $1.80$ at $\delta = 1, 2, 3$, and falls 3 nats behind only after about $5000$, $420$ and $120$ trials. Drawn per accumulator it gives slow errors (14--19\%).
  \item \textbf{Caution shared across the race.} Every draw in the kernel, including $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$'s, is per accumulator. A single draw shared by the race also gives fast errors, because liberal trials are both fast and inaccurate. At 80\% accuracy and $sv = 0$, a shared real threshold $K \sim \operatorname{Uniform}(1.5, 11.5)$ makes errors $15\%$ faster, against $13\%$ slower at a fixed $K = 4$, and rate CV offsets the effect ($+2\%$ at $\mathit{cv} = 0.3$). Such a race no longer factors over accumulators and needs quadrature over the shared draw (16 Gauss--Legendre nodes give about $10^{-8}$ relative accuracy for a bounded draw).
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The exact boundary values $t_0 = 0$, $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0$, $\cvu = 0$, $h = 1$, $K = 1$ and $sv = 0$ are bound exceptions, so they can be fixed without being a limiting approximation.
  \item $sv$ has no upper bound: $sv > v$ ($\beta < 1$) is a valid, very variable rate. Then $p$ lies within rounding of one, so the kernel solves $1 - p$ from its own quantile, $1 - p = Q_{\beta,K}(1-h)$, and returns $F(\infty) = h$ and $S(\infty) = 1-h$ directly. For $\beta < 1$ the complements $1-p$ and $1-q(u) = (1-p) + pS_G(u)$ underflow while $(1-q)^\beta$ is still of order one, so both are held as logarithms, and the incomplete beta takes $\log q$ and $\log(1-q)$ directly. Where the beta quantile underflows, its log is the leading term of $I_x(a,b) = x^a/(aB(a,b))\,(1 + O(x))$. The simulators draw the complement $1-U \sim \BetaDist(\beta, K)$ by inversion on the same log scale. The lower guard $10^{-4}$ keeps a sampled $sv$ off the exact $sv = 0$ branch, which is reachable by fixing $sv$ as a constant. The lower guard $10^{-6}$ on $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ likewise keeps a sampled value off the exact base model.
  \item The mapping layer reports derived quantities: $\beta$, $p$, $\lambda$, the expected total evidence $\beta p/(1-p)$, or $E$ at $sv=0$ (column \texttt{Etot}, infinite at $h=1$), the rate CV $\mathit{cv} = sv/v$ (the rate SD $sv = \mathit{cv}\,v$ in the CV chart). They are not fitted coordinates.
  \item The CV chart is the compiled model \texttt{FRQ\_CV}: the same kernel with $\mathit{cv}$ in the third column, read without dividing by $v$. Its bound, default and exception are $sv$'s.
  \item The likelihood uses Rmath's \texttt{qbeta} and \texttt{qgamma}, a continued-fraction incomplete beta of its own in the general branch, and the package's series and continued-fraction incomplete gamma (\texttt{src/inc\_gamma.h}) in the $sv = 0$ branch, which evaluates only the tail it needs. It is evaluated on the log scale throughout.
\end{itemize}

\section{FRQfade: A Fading Registration Opportunity}\label{sec:fade}
%======================================================================
\texttt{FRQfade()} runs the proper ($h = 1$) \textsc{FRQ} accumulator on the warped time
\begin{equation}
  A(u) = \frac{1 - \e^{-\kappa u}}{\kappa}, \qquad A(u) = u \text{ at } \kappa = 0,
\end{equation}
so that its input intensity is multiplied by $\e^{-\kappa u}$. With $F_1$, $f_1$ and $S_1$ the \textsc{FRQ} functions at $h = 1$ for the same $(K, v, sv, \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}, \cvu)$,
\begin{equation}
  F(u) = F_1\big(A(u)\big), \qquad f(u) = f_1\big(A(u)\big)\,\e^{-\kappa u}, \qquad S(u) = S_1\big(A(u)\big).
\end{equation}
Operational time is capped at $1/\kappa$, so the completion probability is derived rather than fitted:
\begin{equation}
  h = F_1(1/\kappa).
\end{equation}
The expected total evidence a trial can deliver is $\mathbb{E}[W]\,\phi(1/\kappa)$: $v/\kappa$ at $sv = 0$, $\beta q/(1-q)$ at the cap otherwise, and infinite at $\kappa = 0$. The mapping layer reports it as \texttt{Etot}, with $h$, $\beta$, $\lambda$ and $\mathit{cv}$. The simulator draws the $h = 1$ decision time $x$ and returns $u = -\log(1 - \kappa x)/\kappa$, with $x \ge 1/\kappa$ an omission.

The sampled parameters are those of Table~\ref{tab:contract} with $h$ replaced by $\kappa$ (log link, $[10^{-4}, \infty)$ or exactly 0, default 0.5). At $\kappa = 0$ the model is \textsc{FRQ} with $h = 1$. Every \textsc{FRQ} submodel of Table~\ref{tab:submodels} therefore has a fading counterpart on the clock $A(u)$. In particular, $sv = 0$ gives $F(u) = P_K\big(vA(u)\big)$, and $sv = 0$, $K = 1$ gives the exponential with a fading rate.

At $sv = 0$, \textsc{FRQfade} is contained in \textsc{FRQ}. With the exponential clock ($\cvu = 0$), with $M(X) = (1 - \pi_{\mathrm{lo}})P_K(X) + \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}P_1(X)$, \textsc{FRQ}'s $sv = 0$ branch is $M\big(E(1 - \e^{-\lambda u})\big)$, and \textsc{FRQfade} is $M\big((v/\kappa)(1 - \e^{-\kappa u})\big)$. The two are identical at every $u$ when $E = v/\kappa$, $\lambda = \kappa$ and $h = M(v/\kappa)$, which gives the same $v = \lambda E$. For $sv > 0$ the operational clocks differ ($\phi = a(1 - \e^{-\kappa u})$ against $p(1 - \e^{-\lambda u})/(1 - p(1 - \e^{-\lambda u}))$), so the families separate. A claim that fading and a finite evidence ceiling have been distinguished empirically must therefore rest on $sv > 0$ or on a clock other than $\cvu = 0$.
