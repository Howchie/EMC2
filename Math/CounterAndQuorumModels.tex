\begin{abstract}
Poisson count models describe decisions as the accumulation of discrete evidence events to a response criterion. This document develops the quorum counter family (FRQ) from that starting point: a Poisson process on a deterministic operational clock, a Gamma-distributed trial intensity, and a criterion expressed as an event count or its continuous Gamma-threshold extension. Integrating over trial intensity gives a regularized incomplete beta first-passage law. Coordinates for mean initial rate, rate variability, criterion, clock curvature, and eventual completion probability connect the mathematical structure to psychological hypotheses. The family contains the homogeneous Poisson counter, a Gamma-mixed homogeneous counter, a linear pure-birth counter, and counters with bounded cumulative intensity. A two-state criterion mixture and a fading operational clock provide further structured variants. An opponent counter, in which a second Poisson stream removes evidence, gives a Bessel first-passage law with a continuous criterion, a defective form under negative net evidence, and a Wald diffusion limit. Finite reservoirs supply an alternative representation for particular integer shapes, rather than the defining mechanism. Exact distributional identities, differences between event processes, race dependence, and restrictions across experimental conditions determine what these models can explain and what response-time observations can identify.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Poisson Count Model Family}\label{sec:poisson}

Counting theories represent evidence as all-or-none registration events in continuous time. A response occurs when a counter has registered enough events. This connects the quorum stopping rule to recruitment theory \cite{LaBerge1962}, classical choice counters \cite{Pike1966,Pike1973}, and the treatment of elementary processing by Townsend and Ashby \cite{TownsendAshby1983}. The central object here is the distribution of the time to criterion, together with the assumptions that turn separate counters into a choice model.

\subsection{Homogeneous counters}
Let $N(u)$ be a homogeneous Poisson process with rate $v>0$, starting at $N(0)=0$, and let $K\geq1$ be an integer. Decision time is
\begin{equation}
 U=\inf\{u\geq0:N(u)\geq K\}.
\end{equation}
Define the regularized lower incomplete gamma function by
\begin{equation}
 P_K(m)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(K)}\int_0^m x^{K-1}\e^{-x}\dd x.
\end{equation}
The event-count identity then gives
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=P\{N(u)\geq K\}=P_K(vu),\qquad
 f(u)=\frac{v^K u^{K-1}\e^{-vu}}{\Gamma(K)}.\label{eq:erlang}
\end{equation}
Thus $U$ is Erlang with mean $K/v$ and variance $K/v^2$. At $K=1$ it is exponential. Criterion and rate have different distributional effects even when they give the same mean: $\operatorname{CV}^2(U)=1/K$.

\subsection{Operational time and time-dependent rates}\label{sec:operational}
A nonhomogeneous Poisson counter replaces $vu$ with a deterministic, nondecreasing cumulative intensity
\begin{equation}
 M(u)=\int_0^u r(s)\dd s,\qquad M(0)=0.
\end{equation}
For an absolutely continuous clock,
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=P_K(M(u)),\qquad
 f(u)=\frac{M(u)^{K-1}\e^{-M(u)}}{\Gamma(K)}M'(u).\label{eq:nhpp}
\end{equation}
Smith and Van Zandt \cite{SmithVanZandt2000} developed time-dependent Poisson decision counters for two and multiple alternatives, including proportional rate functions and applications to sustained and transient visual channels. Their framework motivates treating operational time as a psychological assumption about the temporal availability of evidence.

An unbounded clock gives eventual completion with probability one. A bounded clock, $M(\infty)=E<\infty$, gives
\begin{equation}
 h=F(\infty)=P_K(E),\qquad P(U=\infty)=1-h.\label{eq:poisson-defect}
\end{equation}
The count eventually available is $\Poisson(E)$, which is finite almost surely but has no fixed upper bound. A finite expected evidence budget therefore does not imply a reservoir containing a fixed number of units. Intrinsic omissions are already possible within the time-dependent Poisson framework.

\subsection{Continuous criteria}
For any real $K>0$, let $Y_K\sim\GammaDist(K,\text{rate }1)$ and define
\begin{equation}
 U=\inf\{u\geq0:M(u)\geq Y_K\}.\label{eq:gamma-threshold}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:nhpp} remains valid. For integer $K$, $Y_K$ is the operational time of the $K$th arrival of a unit-rate Poisson process. For noninteger $K$, it is a continuous relaxation of that arrival level; it is not literally passage to a fractional count of an integer-valued process. The Gamma level already represents Poisson arrival-time randomness at integer $K$, so it is not an additional source of criterion variability in that case.

The family below takes $K\geq1$. Smaller positive shapes still define distributions, but with a locally linear clock the density behaves as $u^{K-1}$ near zero. For $K<1$, this produces a singular leading edge and can make likelihood estimation with a free onset time unbounded.

\section{Gamma-Mixed Quorum Counters}\label{sec:quorum}

\subsection{Generative definition}
\begin{definition}[Quorum counter]
Take independent variables
\begin{equation}
 W\sim\GammaDist(\beta,\text{rate }1),\qquad
 Y_K\sim\GammaDist(K,\text{rate }1),\qquad \beta>0.
\end{equation}
Let $\phi$ be a deterministic nondecreasing operational clock with $\phi(0)=0$. Set
\begin{equation}
 U=\inf\{u\geq0:W\phi(u)\geq Y_K\},\label{eq:quorum-definition}
\end{equation}
with $U=\infty$ when the clock never reaches the level. For integer $K$, conditional on $W$, this is the $K$th arrival time of a Poisson process with cumulative intensity $W\phi(u)$.
\end{definition}
The random variable $W$ is constant throughout a trial. Conditional counts have independent increments, but marginal counts generally do not: all intervals share the same trial intensity. In the real-valued extension, Eq.~\eqref{eq:quorum-definition} defines the finishing time directly.

\begin{proposition}[Integrated first-passage law]\label{prop:beta}
For $q(u)=\phi(u)/(1+\phi(u))$,
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=\E_W[P_K(W\phi(u))]=I_{q(u)}(K,\beta),\label{eq:beta-cdf}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 I_q(a,b)=\frac{1}{B(a,b)}\int_0^q z^{a-1}(1-z)^{b-1}\dd z.
\end{equation}
For a differentiable clock, the density and survivor are
\begin{align}
 f(u)&=\frac{q'(u)q(u)^{K-1}(1-q(u))^{\beta-1}}{B(K,\beta)},\label{eq:beta-density}\\
 S(u)&=1-F(u)=I_{1-q(u)}(\beta,K).\label{eq:beta-survivor}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The ratio $B_*=Y_K/(Y_K+W)$ is $\BetaDist(K,\beta)$. The event $Y_K\leq W\phi(u)$ is equivalent to $B_*\leq\phi(u)/(1+\phi(u))$. Differentiation gives the density, and beta symmetry gives the survivor.
\end{proof}
For integer counts, Gamma mixing also gives the negative-binomial state law
\begin{equation}
 P\{N(u)=n\}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+n)}{\Gamma(\beta)n!}
 (1-q(u))^\beta q(u)^n,\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.\label{eq:nb-count}
\end{equation}
Its mean and variance are $\beta\phi(u)$ and $\beta\phi(u)(1+\phi(u))$, respectively. The count process is defined before stopping; the decision uses its first passage to criterion.

\subsection{A structured family of clocks}\label{sec:clocks}
Introduce $\lambda>0$, $c\geq0$, and $0<p\leq1$, and define
\begin{align}
 G_c(u)&=1-(1+c^2\lambda u)^{-1/c^2}, && c>0,\\
 G_0(u)&=1-\e^{-\lambda u},\\
 q(u)&=pG_c(u),\qquad
 \phi(u)=\frac{pG_c(u)}{1-pG_c(u)}.\label{eq:clock}
\end{align}
Here $G_c$ is a convenient bounded clock coordinate, with $G_c(0)=0$, $G_c(\infty)=1$, and $G_c'(0)=\lambda$. It need not describe the delay of an individual evidence unit. In particular, $c$ controls operational-clock curvature; it is distinct from the trial-rate coefficient of variation defined below.

At $p=1$,
\begin{equation}
 \phi(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 (1+c^2\lambda u)^{1/c^2}-1,&c>0,\\
 \e^{\lambda u}-1,&c=0.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:proper-clock}
\end{equation}
The clock accelerates for $0\leq c<1$, is linear for $c=1$, and decelerates for $c>1$. A linear clock means a constant within-trial rate conditional on $W$; after marginalizing over $W$, the predictive event intensity still depends on count history. At $p<1$, every clock in Eq.~\eqref{eq:clock} saturates at $p/(1-p)$, including the $c=1$ member.

\subsection{Completion probability and evidence budget}\label{sec:defect}
The eventual completion probability and the mean total number of events are
\begin{equation}
 h=I_p(K,\beta),\qquad
 \E[N(\infty)]=\frac{\beta p}{1-p}\quad(p<1).\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Conditional on $W$, the total count is $\Poisson(Wp/(1-p))$; marginally it has the negative-binomial law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nb-count} with $q=p$. Thus the evidence budget is random and unbounded across trials, even though the total count is finite on each trial almost surely. At $p=1$, the operational clock and eventual count are unbounded, and $h=1$.

The defective distribution has a point mass $1-h$ at infinity. Its finite-time density integrates to $h$, not one. Conditional on eventual completion,
\begin{equation}
 F(u\mid U<\infty)=\frac{F(u)}h,\qquad
 f(u\mid U<\infty)=\frac{f(u)}h.\label{eq:conditional-completion}
\end{equation}
Changing $p$ changes the whole finite-time law as well as $h$. Consequently the model is not obtained by multiplying a fixed proper response-time distribution by an independently chosen response probability.

\section{Psychological Coordinates and Boundary Models}\label{sec:coordinates}

\subsection{Criterion, initial rate, variability, and completion}
Define
\begin{equation}
 v=\beta p\lambda,\qquad s_v=\frac{v}{\sqrt\beta},\qquad
 \mathrm{cv}=\frac{s_v}{v}=\beta^{-1/2}.\label{eq:coordinates}
\end{equation}
The initial trial rate is $L_0=W\phi'(0)=Wp\lambda$, so $\E[L_0]=v$ and $\operatorname{SD}(L_0)=s_v$. With $s_v>0$, the coordinates $(K,v,s_v,h,c)$ determine
\begin{equation}
 \beta=(v/s_v)^2,\qquad p=I^{-1}_{h}(K,\beta),\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{v}{\beta p},\label{eq:inverse-chart}
\end{equation}
where $I^{-1}_{h}$ means inversion with respect to the beta probability argument. Uniqueness follows from strict monotonicity of $I_p(K,\beta)$ in $p$. At $h=1$, $p=1$.

A third rate-variability coordinate is the variance-to-mean ratio of the initial rate,
\begin{equation}
 \gamma=\frac{s_v^2}{v}=\mathrm{cv}^2\,v=\frac v\beta,\qquad
 \beta=\frac v\gamma,\qquad \lambda=\frac\gamma p.\label{eq:gamma-coordinate}
\end{equation}
It is the same quantity as the excitation increment of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-birth}, and its role is developed in Section~\ref{sec:scale}.

The $h$ and $\kappa$ coordinates describe two different specifications, developed in Sections~\ref{sec:defect} and~\ref{sec:fade}. The $\alpha$ coordinate replaces $c$ in a third, Section~\ref{sec:accel}. A completion probability of zero is a degenerate always-omitting limit, rather than an interior coordinate with positive initial rate.

These parameters describe a model of evidence, not measurements of neural event counts. Fixing the unit increment establishes an evidence scale: there is no additional free jump size that can be rescaled against both criterion and rate. Nevertheless, rate and criterion can be difficult to separate empirically. Their distinct mathematical roles do not by themselves guarantee precise estimates.

\subsection{The exact zero-variability boundary}\label{sec:zero}
\begin{proposition}[Poisson boundary]
As $s_v\to0$ with $(K,v,h,c)$ fixed, the quorum law has the following limits. At $h=1$,
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=P_K(vu),\label{eq:zero-proper}
\end{equation}
independently of $c$. At $0<h<1$, let $E=P_K^{-1}(h)$ and set $\lambda=v/E$. Then
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=P_K(M(u)),\qquad M(u)=E G_c(u).\label{eq:zero-defective}
\end{equation}
These equations define the $s_v=0$ members exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
As $\beta\to\infty$, $W/\beta\to1$ in probability. If $h=1$, $p=1$, $\lambda=v/\beta$, and $\beta\phi(u)\to vu$. If $h<1$, beta--Gamma convergence gives $\beta p\to E$, while $\lambda=v/(\beta p)\to v/E$. Therefore $\beta\phi(u)\to E G_c(u)$. Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:quorum-definition} gives the asserted limits.
\end{proof}
At this boundary, the deterministic event rate for $h<1$ is
\begin{equation}
 M'(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 v(1+c^2vu/E)^{-1/c^2-1},&c>0,\\
 v\e^{-vu/E},&c=0.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:zero-rate}
\end{equation}
The bounded-clock member is thus a nonhomogeneous Poisson counter, with $N(\infty)\sim\Poisson(E)$. Neither this member nor the ordinary unbounded Poisson counter requires a fixed finite reservoir.

\subsection{Rate SD, rate CV, and time scaling}\label{sec:scale}
The maps $(v,s_v)\leftrightarrow(v,\mathrm{cv})$ describe the same single-accumulator family. Their psychological restrictions differ when rates change across stimuli or alternatives. Holding CV fixed makes $s_v$ proportional to $v$; holding SD fixed makes the weaker accumulator more variable relative to its mean.

\begin{proposition}[Rate scaling]\label{prop:scale}
With $(K,\mathrm{cv},h,c,\pi_{\rm lo})$ fixed,
\begin{equation}
 F(u;v)=F(vu;1),\qquad U_v\overset d=U_1/v.\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For positive CV, $\beta$ and the completion-calibrated $p$ are independent of $v$, and $\lambda=v/(\beta p)$ is proportional to $v$. The clock and its threshold mixture therefore depend on $u$ only through $vu$. At zero CV, the same argument uses the fixed $E$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:zero-defective}, or Eq.~\eqref{eq:zero-proper}.
\end{proof}
In a race with common nondecision time, multiplying all rates by the same factor, with the remaining coordinates in the proposition fixed, rescales decision times and leaves choice probabilities unchanged. Fixed SD does not give this invariance, and unequal nondecision offsets can also change the winning alternative under rate rescaling.

\paragraph{Which coordinate is held fixed.} For a single accumulator the three coordinates $s_v$, $\mathrm{cv}$ and $\gamma$ of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:coordinates} and~\eqref{eq:gamma-coordinate} are reparameterizations of one family. They differ as restrictions when $v$ changes across stimuli or alternatives and the variability coordinate is shared. Each is a member of the family
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{cv}\propto v^{-\delta},\qquad \gamma\propto v^{1-2\delta},\qquad s_v\propto v^{1-\delta},\label{eq:delta-family}
\end{equation}
with fixed CV at $\delta=0$, fixed $\gamma$ at $\delta=1/2$, and fixed SD at $\delta=1$. Only $\delta=0$ satisfies Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}.

On the exponential clock with $h=1$ the fixed-$\gamma$ restriction has a direct reading. There $\lambda=\gamma$ and
\begin{equation}
 F(u;v)=I_{1-\e^{-\gamma u}}\!\left(K,\frac v\gamma\right),\label{eq:fixed-gamma}
\end{equation}
so the clock is common to all accumulators that share $\gamma$, and $v$ enters only through the second shape argument. In the pure-birth representation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-birth}, every accumulator gains the same increment $\gamma$ per event while its initial rate $v$ varies. A change in $v$ then changes the shape of the finishing-time law, not only its scale. Under fixed CV the increment is instead proportional to the initial rate, $\gamma=\mathrm{cv}^2v$, and a change in $v$ only rescales time. Both are restrictions on how rates change across conditions; neither choice separates the excitation and heterogeneity readings of Section~\ref{sec:representations}, which remain the same law. At $h<1$, $\lambda=\gamma/p$ with $p=I^{-1}_h(K,v/\gamma)$, so the shared-$\gamma$ clock rate also varies with $v$ through $p$. At other clocks $\gamma$ remains the initial-rate variance-to-mean ratio, without the excitation reading.

\section{Criterion Variability: A Single-Event State}\label{sec:mixture}

Let the criterion $J$ be independent of trial intensity and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 J=\begin{cases}1,&\text{with probability }\pi_{\rm lo},\\
 K,&\text{with probability }1-\pi_{\rm lo}.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:criterion-mixture}
\end{equation}
Both states use the same evidence-generating process: the same $(\beta,p,\lambda,c)$, with $W$ drawn from the same law. Writing $F_k,f_k,S_k$ for passage to criterion $k$ on that process,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rm mix}(u)=(1-\pi_{\rm lo})F_K(u)+\pi_{\rm lo}F_1(u),\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
with the same weighted sums for density and survivor. For positive rate variability,
\begin{equation}
 F_1(u)=1-(1-q(u))^\beta,\qquad
 f_1(u)=\beta q'(u)(1-q(u))^{\beta-1}.
\end{equation}
For zero variability use $P_k(M(u))$, with $P_1(m)=1-\e^{-m}$.

The mixture's completion probability, rather than either component's probability separately, is $h$. Thus its common clock parameter satisfies
\begin{equation}
 h=(1-\pi_{\rm lo})I_p(K,\beta)+\pi_{\rm lo}I_p(1,\beta).\label{eq:mixture-h}
\end{equation}
The right side is strictly increasing from zero to one. Its unique root lies between $I_h^{-1}(1,\beta)$ and $I_h^{-1}(K,\beta)$. For $s_v=0$, $E$ is instead defined by
\begin{equation}
 h=(1-\pi_{\rm lo})P_K(E)+\pi_{\rm lo}P_1(E),\label{eq:mixture-E}
\end{equation}
and the bounded cumulative intensity is $E G_c(u)$ with $\lambda=v/E$. At $h=1$, the zero-variability mixture uses $M(u)=vu$. Calibrating each component separately to the same $h$ would give different clocks and a different model from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture}.

For $K>1$, a coupled representation takes independent $Y_1\sim\GammaDist(1,1)$ and $Y_{K-1}\sim\GammaDist(K-1,1)$. The ordinary state waits for $Y_1+Y_{K-1}$ in operational time, while the low state stops at $Y_1$. This makes occasional commitment on minimal evidence a specific psychological hypothesis. It does not imply a stimulus-independent guess: the low-state latency and winning probability still depend on evidence rates.

At $\pi_{\rm lo}=0$ the fixed-criterion law is recovered; at $\pi_{\rm lo}=1$ the main criterion $K$ disappears. At $K=1$, the two states coincide and $\pi_{\rm lo}$ disappears. For $K>1$,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rm mix}(u)=\pi_{\rm lo}vu+o(u),\qquad
 f_{\rm mix}(0^+)=\pi_{\rm lo}v.\label{eq:mixture-onset}
\end{equation}
Consequently the mixture changes the onset order even for a large main criterion. A positive onset density makes inference about an unknown onset time nonregular.

Discrete criterion variation connects this construction to the rectangular and offset-geometric criteria considered by Ratcliff and Smith \cite{RatcliffSmith2004}. A two-point distribution concentrates variation on a single-event state and a main criterion. It imposes different predictions from a broad distribution of integer criteria or a continuously varying diffusion starting point. In particular, increasing $K$ can increase the temporal separation of the two states, so their speed--accuracy effects cannot be assumed interchangeable.

\subsection{Geometric criterion excess}\label{sec:omega}
With the coupled representation above, stretch the last criterion unit by $1+\omega$, $\omega\geq0$:
\begin{equation}
 J=Y_{K-1}+(1+\omega)Y_1,\qquad K\geq1.\label{eq:omega-criterion}
\end{equation}
For integer $K$, $(1+\omega)Y_1$ equals in distribution the sum of a $\operatorname{Geometric}$ number $G\geq1$ of unit exponentials with $P(G=g)=(1-r)r^{g-1}$, $r=\omega/(1+\omega)$, so the event-count criterion is $K-1+G$, the offset-geometric criterion. Equation~\eqref{eq:omega-criterion} extends it to real $K$.

Write $s=1-r=1/(1+\omega)$. Conditional on cumulative intensity $m$,
\begin{align}
 P(J\leq m)&=P_K(m)-r^{1-K}\e^{-sm}P_K(rm),\label{eq:omega-conditional}\\
 \frac{\dd}{\dd m}P(J\leq m)&=s\,r^{1-K}\e^{-sm}P_{K-1}(rm),
\end{align}
with $P_0\equiv1$. The two forms of the first line, with $P_{K-1}$ or $P_K$, agree because $P_{K-1}(m)-P_K(m)=m^{K-1}\e^{-m}/\Gamma(K)$. Averaging over $W\sim\GammaDist(\beta,1)$ tilts $W$ to rate $1+s\phi$ and gives
\begin{align}
 F_\omega(u)&=I_{q}(K,\beta)-T(u),\qquad S_\omega(u)=I_{1-q}(\beta,K)+T(u),\\
 T(u)&=r^{1-K}\left(\frac{1-q}{1-rq}\right)^\beta I_{rq}(K,\beta),\\
 f_\omega(u)&=q'\,s\,r^{1-K}\beta\,(1-q)^{\beta-1}(1-rq)^{-\beta-1}I_{rq}(K-1,\beta+1),\label{eq:omega-law}
\end{align}
for any clock, with $q=q(u)$. As $r\to0$, $r^{1-K}I_{rq}(K-1,\beta+1)\to q^{K-1}/(\beta B(K,\beta))$, recovering Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta-density}. At zero rate variability, Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega-conditional} applies with $m=M(u)$.

Completion is calibrated as before, now through
\begin{equation}
 h=I_p(K,\beta)-r^{1-K}\left(\frac{1-p}{1-rp}\right)^\beta I_{rp}(K,\beta),\label{eq:omega-h}
\end{equation}
or Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega-conditional} at $m=E$. Because $Y_K\leq J\leq(1+\omega)Y_K$ in the usual stochastic order, the root lies between the $\omega=0$ root $p_0$ and $p_0(1+\omega)/(1+p_0\omega)$, that is, within $\log(1+\omega)$ of $\operatorname{logit}p_0$; for zero variability, $E\in[E_0,(1+\omega)E_0]$.

The low state of Section~\ref{sec:mixture} removes $Y_{K-1}$ and keeps the stretched unit, so its criterion is $(1+\omega)Y_1$ and
\begin{equation}
 F_{1,\omega}(u)=1-\left(\frac{1-q}{1-rq}\right)^\beta.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:mixture} then mixes the two $\omega$ laws, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega-h} becomes the corresponding mixture. At $K=1$ and $h=1$, the criterion $(1+\omega)Y_1$ is equivalent to multiplying the cumulative intensity by $1/(1+\omega)$, $F_{1,\omega}(u)=1-(1+\phi(u)/(1+\omega))^{-\beta}$. When $\phi$ is proportional to $v$, as for the linear clock $c=1$, the accelerating warp of Section~\ref{sec:accel}, and zero rate variability, this is $v\mapsto v/(1+\omega)$, and $\omega$ and $v$ are not separately defined there.

\section{Choice, Response Time, and Omissions}\label{sec:race}

\subsection{Independent races}
For $R$ alternatives, let $U_i\in(0,\infty]$ be independent decision times, including any independent rate and criterion draws within each accumulator. Let $t_{0i}\geq0$ be nondecision offsets. Response and observed time are
\begin{equation}
 C=\arg\min_i(t_{0i}+U_i),\qquad T=\min_i(t_{0i}+U_i).
\end{equation}
Extend $F_i(u)=0$, $S_i(u)=1$, and $f_i(u)=0$ to $u\leq0$. Then the joint response-time subdensity and choice probability are
\begin{equation}
 g_i(t)=f_i(t-t_{0i})\prod_{j\ne i}S_j(t-t_{0j}),\qquad
 P_i=\int_0^\infty g_i(t)\dd t.\label{eq:race}
\end{equation}
The conditional density given response $i$ is $g_i(t)/P_i$. With $h_i=F_i(\infty)$,
\begin{equation}
 P(T=\infty)=\prod_i(1-h_i),\qquad
 \sum_i P_i=1-\prod_i(1-h_i).\label{eq:race-omissions}
\end{equation}
An accumulator's completion probability $h_i$ is neither its winning probability nor the probability that the race produces any response. If one accumulator is proper, the race has no intrinsic omissions even when its competitors are defective.

At a finite deadline $d$, the probability of no response yet is
\begin{equation}
 P(T>d)=\prod_i S_i(d-t_{0i}).\label{eq:deadline}
\end{equation}
This includes both responses that would arrive later and intrinsic omissions. Their distinction requires an observation model that reflects the response window. If data are conditioned on a response by $d$, the retained joint subdensities must be divided by $1-P(T>d)$.

A random nondecision time is an additional assumption. For a common independent nondecision latency $D$ with distribution $H_D$, and decision-race subdensity $a_i$, the observed subdensity is
\begin{equation}
 g_i(t)=\int_{[0,t]}a_i(t-d)\dd H_D(d).\label{eq:nondecision}
\end{equation}
It must be distinguished from rate or criterion variability when interpreting the leading edge.

\subsection{Shared trial states and dependent races}\label{sec:dependence}
Independence is part of the race definition, rather than a consequence of beta marginal finishing-time laws. If a shared latent state $Z$ makes racers conditionally independent, the correct subdensity is
\begin{equation}
 g_i(t)=\E_Z\left[f_i(t-t_{0i}\mid Z)
                 \prod_{j\ne i}S_j(t-t_{0j}\mid Z)\right].\label{eq:dependent-race}
\end{equation}
In general this is not the product of marginal density and marginal survivors. Similarly, intrinsic omission probability is $\E_Z[\prod_i(1-h_i(Z))]$.

For example, a single caution state can set every accumulator's criterion to one on liberal trials and to its main criterion on cautious trials. Its race law is a mixture of two conditional race laws. Independent low-state draws instead permit trials with one liberal and one cautious accumulator. These specifications can share single-accumulator marginals and still predict different choices and error times.

\section{Heterogeneity, Excitation, and Reservoir Representations}\label{sec:representations}

\subsection{Predictive event intensity}
\begin{proposition}[Marginal counting-process intensity]\label{prop:intensity}
For the Gamma-mixed Poisson process, conditional on its event history $\mathcal H_u$ with $N(u)=n$,
\begin{equation}
 W\mid\mathcal H_u\sim\GammaDist(\beta+n,\text{rate }1+\phi(u)),
\end{equation}
and its predictive event intensity is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_n(u)=(\beta+n)\frac{\phi'(u)}{1+\phi(u)}
          =(\beta+n)\frac{q'(u)}{1-q(u)}.\label{eq:intensity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For event times $u_1,\ldots,u_n\leq u$, the likelihood for $W$ is proportional to
\begin{equation}
 W^n\e^{-W\phi(u)}\prod_j\phi'(u_j).
\end{equation}
Gamma conjugacy gives the posterior. Multiplying its mean by $\phi'(u)$ gives the predictable intensity.
\end{proof}
At $h=1$, so $p=1$ and $\lambda=v/\beta$,
\begin{equation}
 \ell_n(u)=\frac{v(1+n/\beta)}{1+c^2vu/\beta}.\label{eq:proper-intensity}
\end{equation}
For $c=1$, this is the history-dependent intensity of a Gamma-mixed homogeneous Poisson process. For $c=0$ it becomes
\begin{equation}
 \ell_n=v+\gamma n,\qquad \gamma=v/\beta=s_v^2/v.\label{eq:pure-birth}
\end{equation}
This is a time-homogeneous linear pure-birth process with immigration, whose passage law is $I_{1-\e^{-\gamma u}}(K,v/\gamma)$.

The Gamma-mixed counter on the exponential operational clock and the linear pure-birth counter have the same counting-process law after marginalizing $W$. This identity is stronger than equality of one finishing-time distribution. It also shows why an apparent increase in event intensity after an arrival is not, by itself, evidence of causal reinforcement: learning that the trial has a large latent intensity can produce the same predictive law. Wasserman \cite{Wasserman1983} treats the distinction between Gamma heterogeneity and linear contagion and the ambiguity of negative-binomial event counts. His count-distribution comparison should be distinguished from the clock-specific process identity proved here.

The parameter $s_v$ is always the SD of initial trial intensity in the mixed-clock representation. At $c=0$ it can equivalently index excitation through Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-birth}. These readings do not establish two separately identified mechanisms. The $\gamma$ coordinate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma-coordinate} indexes the excitation reading directly; sharing $\gamma$ or $\mathrm{cv}$ across accumulators is a cross-condition restriction (Section~\ref{sec:scale}), not a test of excitation against heterogeneity. A process with a separately Gamma-distributed base rate and an independently chosen excitation coefficient would require its own generative specification; it is not obtained merely by giving $c$ an intermediate value.

\subsection{A finite reservoir as an alternative completion-time law}\label{sec:reservoir}
Suppose $K$ and $N$ are integers with $1\leq K\leq N$. Each of $N$ potential units is independently available with probability $p$ and, if available, registers with delay distribution $G_c$. By time $u$ the registered count is $\Binomial(N,pG_c(u))$. Its quorum latency has
\begin{equation}
 P\{\Binomial(N,q(u))\geq K\}=I_{q(u)}(K,N-K+1).\label{eq:reservoir}
\end{equation}
It agrees with the quorum counter law when $\beta=N-K+1$. This supplies an exact finite-population representation for that subset of shapes. General positive $\beta$ and real $K$ need not yield an integer $N=K+\beta-1$, and the zero-variability Poisson boundary does not require any fixed $N$.

For exponential registration, $p=1$, the reservoir's successive aggregate rates before completion are
\begin{equation}
 N\lambda,(N-1)\lambda,\ldots,(N-K+1)\lambda.
\end{equation}
The counter in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-birth} has rates
\begin{equation}
 \beta\lambda,(\beta+1)\lambda,\ldots,(\beta+K-1)\lambda,
 \qquad \beta=N-K+1.
\end{equation}
These sequences are reversals. In both constructions the total completion time is a sum of independent exponential waiting times. Commutativity of convolution therefore proves
\begin{equation}
 U_{\rm depletion}\overset d=U_{\rm excitation}.\label{eq:reversal}
\end{equation}
For example, $N=K=3$ gives rates $3\lambda,2\lambda,\lambda$ versus $\lambda,2\lambda,3\lambda$, and both have CDF $(1-\e^{-\lambda u})^3$.

The reservoir and counter are different event processes: one has a bounded count and depleting rate; the other can keep generating events and has increasing state-dependent rate. Their completion times, and hence independent race likelihoods built from those times, coincide under the stated mapping. Even their initial aggregate rates differ: $Np\lambda$ for the reservoir versus $\beta p\lambda=v$ for the counter. A finishing-time fit cannot select between these internal mechanisms.

\section{Fading Evidence as a Time Warp}\label{sec:fade}

An alternative specification starts with a proper quorum counter, including an optional criterion mixture, and runs it on the time warp
\begin{equation}
 A_\kappa(u)=\frac{1-\e^{-\kappa u}}{\kappa},\qquad
 A_0(u)=u,\qquad \kappa\geq0.\label{eq:fading-clock}
\end{equation}
Let $F_{\rm base},f_{\rm base},S_{\rm base}$ denote the proper law at the same $(K,v,s_v,c,\pi_{\rm lo})$. Then
\begin{align}
 F_{\rm fade}(u)&=F_{\rm base}(A_\kappa(u)),\\
 f_{\rm fade}(u)&=f_{\rm base}(A_\kappa(u))\e^{-\kappa u},\\
 S_{\rm fade}(u)&=S_{\rm base}(A_\kappa(u)).\label{eq:fading-law}
\end{align}
For positive rate variability, conditional cumulative intensity is $W\phi_{\rm base}(A_\kappa(u))$. Its derivative is $W\phi_{\rm base}'(A_\kappa(u))\e^{-\kappa u}$. Thus the warp attenuates progression through the base clock; a simple exponentially decaying event rate results when the base clock is linear. With an accelerating base clock, the derivative of that base clock also matters.

For $\kappa>0$, warped time ends at $1/\kappa$, giving
\begin{equation}
 h_{\rm fade}=F_{\rm base}(1/\kappa),\qquad
 \E[N(\infty)]=\beta\phi_{\rm base}(1/\kappa)\quad(s_v>0).\label{eq:fading-h}
\end{equation}
At $s_v=0$, expected total count is $v/\kappa$. Here $h$ is derived from the fading rate and the other coordinates, rather than independently specified. At $\kappa=0$, the proper base model is recovered. A base latency $x<1/\kappa$ corresponds to $u=-\log(1-\kappa x)/\kappa$; base latencies at or above the cap give omissions.

\subsection{Exact overlap with a bounded Poisson clock}
With $s_v=0$ and a fixed criterion,
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rm fade}(u)=P_K\left(\frac v\kappa(1-\e^{-\kappa u})\right).\label{eq:poisson-fade}
\end{equation}
This is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:zero-defective} at $c=0$ with
\begin{equation}
 E=v/\kappa,\qquad \lambda=\kappa,\qquad h=P_K(v/\kappa).
\end{equation}
For the criterion mixture, define $H(m)=(1-\pi_{\rm lo})P_K(m)+\pi_{\rm lo}P_1(m)$. The same identity holds with $H$ in place of $P_K$ and $h=H(v/\kappa)$. At $K=1$ this is a single-event counter with exponentially fading rate.

This overlap rules out distinguishing these two parameterizations from response times in that submodel. At positive rate variability the bounded $pG_c/(1-pG_c)$ clock and the warped proper clock generally differ. Any empirical claim of separating fading from a bounded evidence budget must address the exact overlap and state the clock and variability restrictions under which separation is proposed.

\section{Accelerating Evidence as a Time Warp}\label{sec:accel}

A third specification keeps the linear clock, $c=1$, and replaces its time argument by the accelerating warp
\begin{equation}
 A_\alpha(u)=\frac{\e^{\alpha u}-1}{\alpha},\qquad
 A_0(u)=u,\qquad \alpha\geq0.\label{eq:accel-clock}
\end{equation}
With $\lambda=v/(\beta p)$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:inverse-chart} and $x(u)=\lambda A_\alpha(u)$,
\begin{equation}
 q(u)=\frac{p\,x(u)}{1+x(u)},\qquad
 \phi(u)=\frac{p\,x(u)}{1+(1-p)x(u)},\label{eq:accel-q}
\end{equation}
and the law is Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta-cdf} on this $q$:
\begin{align}
 F_{\rm acc}(u)&=F_{c=1}(A_\alpha(u)),\\
 f_{\rm acc}(u)&=f_{c=1}(A_\alpha(u))\e^{\alpha u},\\
 S_{\rm acc}(u)&=S_{c=1}(A_\alpha(u)).\label{eq:accel-law}
\end{align}
Unlike the fading warp, $A_\alpha(\infty)=\infty$, so $h$, $p$ and the evidence budget in Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} are unchanged, and the criterion mixture is calibrated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture-h} or~\eqref{eq:mixture-E} exactly as before. A base latency $x$ corresponds to $u=\log(1+\alpha x)/\alpha$.

\subsection{Rate interpretation}
At $h=1$, conditional on $W$ the event intensity is
\begin{equation}
 r(u\mid W)=W\frac v\beta\,\e^{\alpha u},\label{eq:accel-rate}
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{cv}$ governs differences between trials and $\alpha$ the growth of the rate within a trial, and either can be zero while the other is positive. The initial rate is still $L_0=W v/\beta$, with mean $v$ and CV $\beta^{-1/2}$ for every $h$, since $q'(0)=p\lambda=v/\beta$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity} the predictive intensity is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_n(u)=\frac{v(1+n/\beta)\,\e^{\alpha u}}{1+\frac v{\beta\alpha}(\e^{\alpha u}-1)}.\label{eq:accel-intensity}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact members}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\alpha=0$ is the $c=1$ member of Section~\ref{sec:clocks}, at every $h$ and $\pi_{\rm lo}$.
\item $\alpha=\lambda$ gives $q(u)=p(1-\e^{-\lambda u})$, the $c=0$ member, at every $h$. At $h=1$ this is $\alpha=v/\beta=v\,\mathrm{cv}^2$, where Eq.~\eqref{eq:accel-intensity} reduces to the linear pure-birth intensity $\ell_n=v+\alpha n$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pure-birth}.
\item $s_v=0$, $h=1$ gives the accelerating nonhomogeneous Poisson counter
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=P_K\!\left(v\frac{\e^{\alpha u}-1}{\alpha}\right),\label{eq:accel-poisson}
\end{equation}
with intensity $v\e^{\alpha u}$; unlike Eq.~\eqref{eq:zero-proper}, the clock does not drop out at zero variability. At $s_v=0$, $h<1$, $F(u)=P_K(E\,x(u)/(1+x(u)))$ with $\lambda=v/E$.
\end{itemize}
In a race, the $c=0$ correspondence holds accumulator by accumulator: a common $\alpha$ reproduces a race of $c=0$ accumulators only if $\alpha=\lambda_i$ for every alternative $i$.

\subsection{Time scale and tails}
Because $\alpha$ has units of inverse time, Proposition~\ref{prop:scale} becomes
\begin{equation}
 F(u;v,\alpha)=F(vu;1,\alpha/v),\label{eq:accel-scale}
\end{equation}
so a common change of rate rescales decision times only if $\alpha$ changes in proportion.

At $K=1$ and $h=1$, the leading edge is
\begin{equation}
 -\log S(u)=vu+\tfrac12 v\left(\alpha-v\,\mathrm{cv}^2\right)u^2+O(u^3),\label{eq:accel-onset}
\end{equation}
so acceleration and rate variability enter the onset hazard slope with opposite signs.

For $h=1$, $s_v>0$ and $\alpha>0$, $1-q(u)\sim(\alpha\beta/v)\e^{-\alpha u}$, and
\begin{equation}
 S(u)\sim\frac{(\alpha\beta/v)^\beta}{\beta B(K,\beta)}\e^{-\alpha\beta u},\label{eq:accel-tail}
\end{equation}
an exponential tail with rate $\alpha/\mathrm{cv}^2$, against the power tail of Eq.~\eqref{eq:proper-tail} at $\alpha=0$. At $s_v=0$ the tail of Eq.~\eqref{eq:accel-poisson} is doubly exponential. At $0<h<1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:defective-tail} applies with $1-G_1(A_\alpha(u))=1/(1+x(u))$, so the finite completions have an exponential tail with rate $\alpha$, where the $\alpha=0$ member has the $1/u$ tail with no conditional mean.

\section{Opponent Counters: Signed Poisson Evidence}\label{sec:opponent}

The counters above only add evidence. An opponent counter also removes it. Accumulator $i$ receives two independent homogeneous Poisson streams, $N_{+}(u)$ with rate $a=\lambda_+>0$ and $N_{-}(u)$ with rate $b=\lambda_-\geq0$, and its signed evidence is
\begin{equation}
 X(u)=N_{+}(u)-N_{-}(u),\qquad X(0)=0,\qquad
 U_K=\inf\{u\geq0:X(u)\geq K\},\quad K=1,2,\ldots\label{eq:opponent}
\end{equation}
This is a continuous-time simple random walk, whose first-passage times have a long history \cite{Feller1971,Masuda1988}. Because $X$ is integer valued, Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent} is a first passage only at integer $K$: for noninteger $K$ it is passage to $\lceil K\rceil$. Real criteria are introduced below through the criterion-indexed law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-laplace}, not through Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent}.
The negative stream acts within its own accumulator; it is not inhibition received from a competitor, and racing accumulators remain independent in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:race}. In an increment $\dd u$, $\E[\dd X]=(a-b)\dd u$ and $\Var(\dd X)=(a+b)\dd u$, so the net rate $v=a-b$ and the variance rate $D=a+b$ satisfy $D\geq|v|$. The difference of the rates sets the mean progress and their sum sets the within-trial fluctuation.

\subsection{First-passage law for a continuous criterion}
Let $I_K$ denote the modified Bessel function of the first kind of real order $K>0$. For integer $K$ the first-passage subdensity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent} is, and for real $K>0$ the continuous-criterion subdensity is defined to be,
\begin{equation}
 f(u)=\frac Ku\left(\frac ab\right)^{K/2}\e^{-(a+b)u}I_K\!\left(2u\sqrt{ab}\right),\qquad u>0.\label{eq:opponent-density}
\end{equation}
Expanding the Bessel series gives the equivalent Poisson mixture
\begin{equation}
 f(u)=\frac Ku\sum_{j=0}^\infty\pi_j(bu)\,\gamma_{K+j}(au),\qquad
 \pi_j(m)=\frac{m^j\e^{-m}}{j!},\quad
 \gamma_s(x)=\frac{x^s\e^{-x}}{\Gamma(s+1)}.\label{eq:opponent-mixture}
\end{equation}
For integer $K$ the sum is $P\{X(u)=K\}$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-mixture} is the hitting-time theorem for a walk with unit upward jumps: $f(u)=(K/u)P\{X(u)=K\}$. For every real $K>0$ the density has Laplace transform
\begin{equation}
 \E\!\left[\e^{-sU};U<\infty\right]=\e^{-K\psi(s)},\qquad
 \psi(s)=\log\frac{a+b+s+\sqrt{(a+b+s)^2-4ab}}{2a}.\label{eq:opponent-laplace}
\end{equation}
Since $\psi'(s)=[(a+b+s)^2-4ab]^{-1/2}$ is completely monotone, $\psi$ is a Bernstein function, with killing $\psi(0)>0$ when $a<b$. The finishing time as a function of criterion, $K\mapsto U_K$, is therefore a (possibly killed) subordinator. This is the sense in which a noninteger criterion is defined: the integer-valued path does not take fractional steps, but the criterion-indexed finishing times $U_K$ form a process that agrees with whole-unit passage at integer levels and interpolates between them. As for the counters of Section~\ref{sec:poisson}, the family is taken with $K\geq1$: the onset of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-onset} behaves as $u^{K-1}$, so $K<1$ gives a singular leading edge and can make the likelihood with a free onset time unbounded.

Write $P_s$ and $Q_s=1-P_s$ for the regularized incomplete gamma functions of Section~\ref{sec:poisson}, and $H_{\nu,\delta}(x)=P\{\chi'^2_\nu(\delta)\leq x\}=\sum_j\pi_j(\delta/2)P_{\nu/2+j}(x/2)$ for the noncentral chi-squared CDF with $\nu$ degrees of freedom and noncentrality $\delta$. The CDF is the sum of two such probabilities,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 F(u)&=H_{2K,\,2bu}(2au)+\left(\frac ab\right)^KH_{2K+2,\,2au}(2bu)\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\pi_j(bu)\,P_{K+j}(au)
 +\left(\frac ab\right)^K\sum_{j=0}^\infty\pi_j(au)\,P_{K+1+j}(bu),
\end{split}\label{eq:opponent-cdf}
\end{equation}
valid for real $K>0$, and in Poisson-mixture form the survivor is
\begin{equation}
 S(u)=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\pi_j(bu)\,Q_{K+j}(au)
 -\left(\frac ab\right)^K\sum_{j=0}^\infty\pi_j(au)\,P_{K+1+j}(bu).\label{eq:opponent-survivor}
\end{equation}
The second sum has order $K+1+j$, not $K+j$; with a common order the expression does not differentiate to Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-density}. Both orders are real for real $K$. Equation~\eqref{eq:opponent-survivor} vanishes as $u\to\infty$ when $a\geq b$ and tends to the omission mass otherwise. For integer $K$ the first sum is $P\{X(u)<K\}$, and the second removes the paths that are below $K$ at time $u$ after having reached it. Both terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-cdf} are positive, so it determines $F$ to full relative precision at the leading edge. In the upper tail the two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-survivor} share the decay $\e^{-(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2u}$ and their difference is smaller than either by a factor of order $1/u$, so the survivor is formed by a difference there, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-cdf} is the better form before the median.

\subsection{Completion, rate exchange, and limits}
Setting $s=0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-laplace} gives
\begin{equation}
 h=P(U<\infty)=\begin{cases}1,&a\geq b,\\(a/b)^K,&a<b.\end{cases}\label{eq:opponent-h}
\end{equation}
For $a<b$, conditioning on completion exchanges the rates:
\begin{equation}
 f(u;a,b)=h\,f(u;b,a),\qquad S(u;a,b)=(1-h)+h\,S(u;b,a).\label{eq:opponent-swap}
\end{equation}
Rare completions under negative net evidence thus have the latency law of the positive-drift counter with the streams exchanged, and the defective law needs no separate form. At $a=b$ completion is certain but the mean is infinite.

For $a>b$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[U]=\frac K{a-b},\qquad \Var(U)=\frac{K(a+b)}{(a-b)^3},\qquad
 \operatorname{CV}^2(U)=\frac{a+b}{K(a-b)}.\label{eq:opponent-moments}
\end{equation}
These are the mean and variance of a Wald time with distance $K$, drift $v$, and variance rate $D$, but the laws differ at every finite event scale. The third derivative of $K\psi$ at zero gives the third cumulant, and with $r=D/v\geq1$ the skewness:
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_3(U)=\frac{K(3D^2-v^2)}{v^5},\qquad
 \gamma_1(U)=\frac{3r^2-1}{\sqrt K\,r^{3/2}}.\label{eq:opponent-skew}
\end{equation}
The Wald with the same mean and variance has $\gamma_1=3\sqrt{r/K}$, so
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{1,\rm Wald}-\gamma_{1}(U)=\frac1{\sqrt K\,r^{3/2}}>0.\label{eq:opponent-skew-gap}
\end{equation}
The opponent counter is always less skewed than its moment-matched Wald. The gap shrinks as opposing activity grows relative to net drift ($r\to\infty$) or as the criterion grows, the two routes to the diffusion limit. It is largest at $b=0$, where $r=1$ and $\gamma_1=2/\sqrt K$, the Gamma skewness. In a race these are properties of each accumulator's finishing time; the winning-time distributions inherit them only through Eq.~\eqref{eq:race}. Giving each event magnitude $\epsilon$ with $a,b=D/(2\epsilon^2)\pm v/(2\epsilon)$ and criterion $d/\epsilon$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-laplace} converges to the Wald transform $\exp[(d/D)(v-\sqrt{v^2+2Ds})]$, including its defective form for $v<0$.

As $u\downarrow0$,
\begin{equation}
 f(u)\sim\frac{a^K}{\Gamma(K)}u^{K-1},\qquad F(u)\sim\frac{(au)^K}{\Gamma(K+1)},\label{eq:opponent-onset}
\end{equation}
so the onset is that of a Gamma counter with rate $a$; the negative stream first enters at relative order $u$. The leading edge is therefore power-law, against the exponentially suppressed onset of a Wald. As $u\to\infty$ with $a,b>0$,
\begin{equation}
 f(u)\sim\frac{K(a/b)^{K/2}}{\sqrt{4\pi\sqrt{ab}}}\,u^{-3/2}\,\e^{-(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2u},\label{eq:opponent-tail}
\end{equation}
an exponentially tempered $u^{-3/2}$ tail whose rate $(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2$ vanishes at $a=b$.
Integrating the Hankel expansion $\e^{-z}I_K(z)\sim(2\pi z)^{-1/2}\sum_k(-1)^k\alpha_k(K)z^{-k}$, $\alpha_k(K)=\prod_{j=1}^k\big(4K^2-(2j-1)^2\big)/(k!\,8^k)$, term by term over $(u,\infty)$ gives the survivor
\begin{equation}
 S(u)\sim K\left(\frac ab\right)^{K/2}(2\pi z)^{-1/2}\sum_{k\geq0}(-1)^k\alpha_k(K)\,z^{-k}E_{3/2+k}(cu),\qquad z=2u\sqrt{ab},\quad c=(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2,\label{eq:opponent-hankel-survivor}
\end{equation}
with $E_p$ the generalised exponential integral. Because $z$ grows with $u$, the truncation error at $u$ bounds it at every later time, so once $z\gg K^2$ a handful of terms gives $S$ to rounding however large the rates are, and with no cancellation between the two sums of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-survivor} near $a=b$.

At $b=0$ the counter is the continuous-criterion homogeneous counter of Eq.~\eqref{eq:erlang}, $F(u)=P_K(au)$. For real $K$ this coincides with the Gamma-level extension of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma-threshold}: both constructions give the Gamma subordinator in criterion, and they first differ when a negative stream is present.

Three coordinate systems describe the same law.

In the ratio coordinates, opposing activity is proportional to net evidence, so
\begin{equation}
 U(v,\rho)\overset{d}{=}U(1,\rho)/v,\qquad
 \E[U]=K/v,\qquad \operatorname{CV}^2(U)=(1+2\rho)/K.\label{eq:opponent-ratio}
\end{equation}
At fixed $(K,\rho)$, net rate is a pure time scale, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}: a common rescaling of every racer's $v$ changes decision-time units and leaves choice probabilities unchanged when nondecision times are common. Weaker alternatives then fluctuate less.

In the background coordinates, both streams carry a common activity $\lambda_0$ that does not depend on the signal, and the signal adds $v$ to the positive stream only. Then
\begin{equation}
 U(v,\lambda_0)\overset{d}{=}U(1,\lambda_0/v)/v,\qquad
 \operatorname{CV}^2(U)=\frac{v+2\lambda_0}{Kv},\label{eq:opponent-background}
\end{equation}
so a change of net rate at fixed background is not a pure time scale. A weaker racer has relatively more noise and a broader standardized latency law, approaching a diffusion with a fixed variance rate when $\lambda_0\gg v$. The two restricted coordinate systems coincide on a single racer through $\rho=\lambda_0/v$, and differ in what they hold constant across racers and conditions. Both keep $v>0$ and hence $h=1$. Intrinsic omissions require the unrestricted rates with $\lambda_-$ exceeding $\lambda_+$ for some racer, and then Eq.~\eqref{eq:race-omissions} applies with $h_i$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:opponent-h}.

\subsection{Races}
The race likelihood of Eq.~\eqref{eq:race} applies with $f_i$ and $S_i$ from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:opponent-density} and~\eqref{eq:opponent-survivor}. Each racer has its own pair of streams, so the model is neither a single difference process between two absorbing boundaries nor a race in which one alternative's events decrement its competitors. Those specifications share the opponent intuition but define different joint processes. When racers differ only in $v$ under shared ratio coordinates, they differ by rate scaling and Eq.~\eqref{eq:elasticity} governs the ordering of error and correct latencies. Under shared background coordinates they differ in standardized shape as well.

\section{Distributional Predictions and Identification}\label{sec:identification}

\subsection{Leading edges, tails, and moments}
For a fixed criterion, $q(u)\sim(v/\beta)u$ at onset. Hence, for positive rate variability,
\begin{equation}
 F(u)\sim\frac{(vu/\beta)^K}{K B(K,\beta)},\qquad
 f(u)\sim\frac{(v/\beta)^K}{B(K,\beta)}u^{K-1}.\label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}
At zero variability the leading CDF is $(vu)^K/\Gamma(K+1)$. At $K=1$, $f(0^+)=v$ regardless of $h,s_v,c$. At larger $K$, the onset order is $K$, but its coefficient depends on $\beta$ as well as $v$. The low-state mixture changes that order to one through Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture-onset}.

For $h=1$, $s_v>0$, and $c>0$, beta-tail asymptotics give
\begin{equation}
 S(u)\sim\frac{(c^2\lambda u)^{-\beta/c^2}}{\beta B(K,\beta)}.\label{eq:proper-tail}
\end{equation}
Thus positive moments of order $r$ exist precisely for $r<\beta/c^2$. At $c=0$, the tail instead decays as $\e^{-\beta\lambda u}=\e^{-vu}$, up to a positive constant. The ordinary Poisson boundary has an exponential tail for every $c$.

At $0<h<1$ with positive rate variability,
\begin{equation}
 h-F(u)\sim
 \frac{p^K(1-p)^{\beta-1}}{B(K,\beta)}\big(1-G_c(u)\big).\label{eq:defective-tail}
\end{equation}
Consequently the finite completion-time distribution has a power tail with exponent $1/c^2$ for $c>0$, and an exponential tail for $c=0$. The same exponents hold at zero variability with a bounded clock. Unconditional moments are infinite whenever there is positive mass at infinity; moment statements for a defective law must refer to $U\mid U<\infty$. Conditional positive moments then require $r<1/c^2$ for $c>0$. These tail results also hold for the two-state mixture, whose components share the relevant exponent.

For the proper, fixed-criterion, linear-clock member, write $U=Y_K/L$, where $Y_K$ is independent of a trial rate
\begin{equation}
 L\sim\GammaDist(\beta,\text{rate }\beta/v).
\end{equation}
For $0\leq r<\beta$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[U^r]=\left(\frac\beta v\right)^r
 \frac{\Gamma(K+r)\Gamma(\beta-r)}{\Gamma(K)\Gamma(\beta)}.\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \E[U]=\frac{K\beta}{v(\beta-1)},\qquad
 \operatorname{CV}^2(U)=\left(1+\frac1K\right)
 \frac{\beta-1}{\beta-2}-1,\label{eq:cv-time}
\end{equation}
with the mean requiring $\beta>1$ and the variance requiring $\beta>2$. The initial-rate CV and the response-time CV are distinct quantities.

\subsection{Fast and slow errors}\label{sec:errors}
With common nondecision time, define the defective-time hazard $\eta_i(u)=f_i(u)/S_i(u)$. For any pair of alternatives $e$ and $a$, the survivor factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:race} cancel to give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g_e(t)}{g_a(t)}=\frac{\eta_e(u)}{\eta_a(u)},\qquad u=t-t_0.\label{eq:hazard-ratio}
\end{equation}
After conditioning on response identity, the ratio is multiplied by $P_a/P_e$. If the hazard ratio is nondecreasing over time, error latencies are later than correct latencies in monotone likelihood-ratio order, and therefore in stochastic order. A nonincreasing ratio gives the reverse ordering. These are sufficient conditions for quantile ordering; quantile ordering alone does not require a monotone density ratio. If the ratio changes direction, some conditional quantiles can cross.

When racers differ only by the rate scaling of Eq.~\eqref{eq:scale}, let $\eta_*$ be the unit-rate hazard and
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon(x)=\frac{\dd\log\eta_*(x)}{\dd\log x}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\log(\eta_e(u)/\eta_a(u))}{\dd\log u}
 =\epsilon(v_eu)-\epsilon(v_au).\label{eq:elasticity}
\end{equation}
For $v_e<v_a$, a nonincreasing $\epsilon$ gives slow errors by the preceding ordering result. Establishing that condition requires analysis of the particular member; a finite parameter grid cannot prove it for the entire family.

Gamma rate mixing can select unusually favorable error trials and alter the tails of conditional response distributions. Equal SD and equal CV impose different selection effects because the slower accumulator has higher relative variability under equal SD. A defective competitor may mainly win early, before its opportunity to accrue evidence fades. A low-criterion state can also concentrate error responses near onset. These mechanisms can produce fast or slow error patterns under suitable parameters; none is a universal sign rule independent of criterion, clock, race dependence, and observation window. An exponential race with fixed rates and unit criteria provides an exact neutral case with identical conditional response-time distributions.

\subsection{Exact degeneracies and practical confounding}
At $h=1$, $K=1$, and $s_v>0$,
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-(1+c^2vu/\beta)^{-\beta/c^2},&c>0,\\
 1-\e^{-vu},&c=0.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:unit-confounding}
\end{equation}
For $c>0$, only $\beta/c^2$ is identified by the finishing-time law. At $c=0$, $\beta$ drops out entirely. At $s_v=0,h=1$, $c$ drops out for every $K$. Criterion-mixture degeneracies occur at $K=1$ and $\pi_{\rm lo}=1$ as described in Section~\ref{sec:mixture}. These are exact distributional facts, not effects that additional trials in the same design can resolve.

Outside these boundaries, parameters may remain difficult to distinguish over a finite observation window. Clock curvature, trial-rate variation, criterion variability, and nondecision variability can all broaden observed latencies. Early data constrain onset behavior; long windows and omission information constrain completion and tails; joint choice and response-time distributions constrain the competition between accumulators. Which coordinates can be estimated precisely depends on the experimental design and the restrictions shared across its conditions.

\section{Relationship to Psychological Theory and Literature}\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Classical counters and time-dependent evidence}
The homogeneous Poisson race is exactly nested by $s_v=0$, $h=1$, and $\pi_{\rm lo}=0$, with $v$ equal to event rate and integer $K$ equal to the criterion count. This is an exact submodel relation, rather than a similarity of distributional shapes. Real-valued $K$ extends its finishing-time family through Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma-threshold}.

The relationship to the general time-dependent counter framework of Smith and Van Zandt \cite{SmithVanZandt2000} has two levels. At zero rate variability, the quorum clock directly specifies a deterministic cumulative intensity. With Gamma mixing, the trial-conditional process is still a time-dependent Poisson counter, but its cumulative intensity is random. The parametric clocks here impose specific restrictions; the general deterministic-clock framework permits other temporal profiles and proportional-rate constraints. The following result gives a further relation at the level of observable finishing times.

\begin{proposition}[A deterministic-clock representation of a marginal law]\label{prop:mimic}
Let $F$ be a continuous finishing-time CDF on $[0,\infty)$ with $F(0)=0$, possibly defective, and $F(u)<1$ at finite $u$. For any fixed integer $k\geq1$, define
\begin{equation}
 M_*(u)=P_k^{-1}(F(u)).\label{eq:mimic-clock}
\end{equation}
Then a deterministic-clock Poisson counter with criterion $k$ has exactly CDF $F$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$P_k$ is strictly increasing from zero to one, so $M_*$ is nondecreasing and $P_k(M_*(u))=F(u)$. This is the time-changed Poisson arrival identity. When $F$ is smooth with positive density away from onset, its clock has event rate
\begin{equation}
 M_*'(u)=\frac{f(u)}{M_*(u)^{k-1}\e^{-M_*(u)}/\Gamma(k)}.
\end{equation}
If $F(\infty)=h<1$, the clock ends at $P_k^{-1}(h)$; otherwise it is unbounded.
\end{proof}
This construction includes the quorum criterion mixture. Applying it separately to each independent racer preserves every marginal survivor and subdensity, hence the complete joint choice--time likelihood and omission probability. It does not preserve internal count histories or necessarily satisfy restrictions linking rates across alternatives or experimental conditions. The result is a distributional construction, not evidence that Smith and Van Zandt's specific psychological applications used these clocks. It establishes why one marginal response-time distribution cannot identify Gamma intensity mixing against an unrestricted deterministic clock.

\subsection{Rate dependence and the Ratcliff--Smith comparison}
Ratcliff and Smith \cite{RatcliffSmith2004} varied rates by allocating a fixed total between two counters:
\begin{equation}
 B\sim\BetaDist(a,b),\qquad L_1=rB,\qquad L_2=r(1-B).
\end{equation}
Thus $\operatorname{Cov}(L_1,L_2)=-r^2\Var(B)$. Their model also included nondecision variability and rectangular or offset-geometric criterion variation. Their Appendix combines a common criterion offset with both response criteria. Independent Gamma-rate racers and independent two-state criterion draws make different joint assumptions. The complete model they examined is therefore not an exact submodel of the independent quorum race.

Their counter fits had difficulty with distributional shape and speed--accuracy error patterns; geometric criteria improved on rectangular criteria without resolving the comparison. These findings concern their specified mechanisms and constraints. They neither settle the performance of this broader family nor establish that its extra flexibility resolves those empirical difficulties. That requires a comparison under explicit restrictions across conditions.

There is a useful mathematical bridge between the rate specifications. For independent $L_1\sim\GammaDist(a,\text{rate }b_0)$ and $L_2\sim\GammaDist(b,\text{rate }b_0)$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{L_1}{L_1+L_2}\sim\BetaDist(a,b),\qquad
 L_1+L_2\sim\GammaDist(a+b,\text{rate }b_0),
\end{equation}
and proportion and total are independent. Conditioning this total to be $r$ produces the fixed-total Beta allocation. Without that conditioning the total varies, allowing both racers to be fast or slow together. This bridge concerns common-rate Gamma distributions; it is not a general equivalence of arbitrary Gamma-rate races and fixed-total allocations.

\subsection{Discriminating mechanisms across experimental conditions}
Van Zandt, Colonius, and Proctor \cite{VanZandtColoniusProctor2000} compared Poisson race and diffusion models under response deadlines, response-frequency manipulations, and stimulus eccentricity. They found that constrained race models could account for their perceptual-matching data at least as well as the diffusion model. Ratcliff and Smith \cite{RatcliffSmith2004} challenged aspects of the deadline comparison, including how deadline-controlled responding was modeled. Together these studies motivate treating experimental constraints and the observation rule as part of a model comparison.

A useful speed--accuracy comparison holds the stimulus-to-rate relation and its chosen SD or CV restriction constant across instruction conditions, while allowing explicitly motivated changes in criterion or criterion-state probability. Allowing $h$, rate variability, and clock shape to change freely with every instruction would weaken that comparison. Stimulus difficulty and response bias should likewise be assigned to parameters through psychological hypotheses, rather than through whichever changes improve a fit. Distributional evidence should include accuracy, correct and error quantiles, and their changes across conditions, alongside deadline survival or omission information when available.

The family unifies established event-count mechanisms within a tractable first-passage structure. A larger set of possible response-time shapes does not establish a unique account of evidence generation. Exact process identities, finishing-time equivalences, and the dependence assumptions of the race determine which psychological interpretations remain distinguishable. Evidence about intermediate counts or independently constrained temporal processing can address ambiguities that choice and finishing times alone leave unresolved.
